# Appendix H (continued): Referral, Safeguarding and Crisis Pathways

## Worked Guidance

It is useful to distinguish the level of mechanism from the level of description. To say that an adolescent is withdrawn, argumentative or disengaged is to describe behaviour; it is not yet to explain it. Explanation begins when we can specify the process that produces the behaviour: the appraisal that precedes it, the reinforcement that follows it, the skill that is missing, the relationship that is being protected, or the developmental demand that exceeds current capacity. Counsellors earn their professional authority precisely at this point, because a mechanism can be intervened with, whereas a description can only be repeated. Throughout this book, therefore, each topic is pressed until it yields mechanisms: what changes, through what process, under what conditions, for which adolescents, and with what implications for the next counselling session.

Trajectories are the signature concept of developmental science. Two adolescents can arrive at the same examination result, the same diagnosis or the same family argument by entirely different routes, and two adolescents who look identical today may be heading in opposite directions. Assessment that captures only the present level of functioning therefore misses the clinically decisive information, which is direction and rate of change. Is this difficulty new, stable, worsening or improving? What changed just before it? What has already been tried, by the adolescent, the family and the school, and with what effect? A counsellor who habitually asks about trajectory writes better formulations, sets more realistic goals and notices recovery earlier than a counsellor who works from snapshots.

## Common Errors

Three errors recur when these materials are used. The first is mechanical completion: every box filled, no judgement exercised, and the resulting document mistaken for an assessment. The second is premature sharing: information passed to schools or agencies before the adolescent understands and, where lawful, agrees to what will be shared and why. The third is fossilisation: forms completed once and never revisited although the adolescent, the family and the difficulty have all changed. Each error is prevented by the same discipline — date everything, review everything against trajectory, and let the adolescent see and question what is recorded about them wherever policy permits.

## Quality Standard

Work in this appendix reaches graduate standard when a reader who does not know the case can reconstruct, from the documents alone, what was known, what was decided, on what developmental reasoning, with whose agreement, and what happened next. Audit your own files against that standard twice each term.
