# Appendix B (continued): Adolescent Case Portfolio

## Worked Guidance

Development rarely moves in a straight line. It advances through the continuous transaction between a changing young person and a changing environment, so that each new capacity reorganises the meaning of earlier experience. A memory skill that was adequate in primary school becomes insufficient when secondary school demands independent revision; a family rule that felt protective at ten feels restrictive at fifteen. The counsellor who understands this transactional quality stops asking only what is wrong with the adolescent and begins asking what fit between person and environment has been disturbed, what new demand has appeared, and what support would restore movement. That single shift, from locating difficulty inside the young person to locating it in a developing system, changes assessment, language, goals and the adults who must be involved in the work.

Developmental cascades describe how functioning in one domain spills into others over time. Early reading difficulty becomes avoidance of text, which becomes restricted knowledge, which becomes low academic self-concept, which becomes disengagement, which becomes association with other disengaged peers. The same logic works positively: one experience of competence, publicly recognised, can raise willingness to attempt the next challenge. The practical lesson is that modest, well-placed interventions can have disproportionate long-term value when they interrupt a negative cascade early or start a positive one, and that counsellors should think about the second and third consequences of any difficulty, not only the presenting one.

## Common Errors

Three errors recur when these materials are used. The first is mechanical completion: every box filled, no judgement exercised, and the resulting document mistaken for an assessment. The second is premature sharing: information passed to schools or agencies before the adolescent understands and, where lawful, agrees to what will be shared and why. The third is fossilisation: forms completed once and never revisited although the adolescent, the family and the difficulty have all changed. Each error is prevented by the same discipline — date everything, review everything against trajectory, and let the adolescent see and question what is recorded about them wherever policy permits.

## Quality Standard

Work in this appendix reaches graduate standard when a reader who does not know the case can reconstruct, from the documents alone, what was known, what was decided, on what developmental reasoning, with whose agreement, and what happened next. Audit your own files against that standard twice each term.
