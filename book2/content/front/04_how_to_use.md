# How to Use This Book

## For students

- Read the Learning Objectives before each chapter and the Chapter Summary after it; together they define what mastery of the chapter means.
- Complete the Practice Activities before attempting the Practice Laboratory; the laboratory assumes the concepts have already been handled actively.
- Keep a developmental timeline notebook throughout the course. Many chapters will ask you to add to it, and it becomes a revision instrument at examination.
- Use the Review Questions in writing, not in thought. Developmental reasoning improves when it is externalised, dated and revised.

## For lecturers

- Each chapter supplies one two-hour lecture and one two-hour seminar without further preparation: Sections 1 to 6 for the lecture, Sections 15 to 17 and Appendix B cases for the seminar.
- The Advanced Seminar Exercises are designed for assessed discussion and may be set as short coursework.
- Chapter 58 and Appendix I contain the course assessment blueprint, portfolio requirements and study guidance aligned to the 1.6-unit weight of the course.

## Conventions

Ages are given in years unless otherwise stated. *Adolescence* is used for the period from approximately ten to nineteen years, with early adolescence approximately ten to thirteen, middle adolescence fourteen to seventeen and late adolescence eighteen to nineteen and into the transition to adulthood, following international health usage while noting that all such boundaries are conveniences rather than natural joints. Case material is composite and fictional throughout.
