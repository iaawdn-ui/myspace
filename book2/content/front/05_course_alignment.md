# Course Alignment: CAC. 702 in the MA in Children and Adolescent Counseling

CAC. 702 Adolescent Development carries 1.6 units in the Master of Arts in Children and Adolescent Counseling. It builds directly on CAC. 701, in which students studied the fundamental theories of counselling, and it provides the developmental foundation required by the later courses of the programme, including play and expressive approaches, school counselling, family work, trauma-focused practice, group work and supervised practicum courses in the CAC. 710 to CAC. 760 sequence and the electives numbered CAC. 722 to CAC. 729.

## Course description

This course examines adolescence as a developmental period: its biological foundations in puberty and brain development; its cognitive transformations; the formation of identity, self and values; emotional and relational reorganisation with parents, peers and romantic partners; the developmental meaning of risk, adversity and resilience; and the cultural, socioeconomic and institutional contexts in which contemporary adolescents grow up. Throughout, developmental knowledge is translated into counselling assessment, formulation, intervention, prevention and advocacy for young people aged approximately ten to nineteen.

## Learning outcomes

On completing CAC. 702 the student will be able to: (1) explain the major theories of adolescent development and evaluate their evidence; (2) describe typical trajectories and individual differences across biological, cognitive, emotional, social and moral domains; (3) conduct a developmental assessment and write a testable formulation; (4) match counselling methods to developmental level and engage families, schools and other professionals appropriately; (5) design developmentally informed prevention and early intervention; and (6) practise within the ethical, cultural and safeguarding requirements of work with minors.

## Assessment

Assessment, detailed in Chapter 58, combines a written examination, a developmental case portfolio built from Appendix B, assessed seminar exercises from the chapters, and a supervised practice reflection using the templates of Appendix G, weighted to reflect the 1.6-unit value of the course.
