# Course-to-Presentation-Style Mapping
## Master of Marriage and Family Therapy — Triune Biblical University Global Extension, USA

Each course below is matched to the presentation styles (from `01-Presentation-Style-Prompts.md`) that fit it best, with a ready-to-use, course-specific prompt.

---

## General Setup Line (prepend to every prompt)

> "Audience: Master's students in Marriage and Family Therapy at Triune Biblical University Global Extension. Tone: professional, warm, clinically grounded. Length: [10–15] minutes. Narration: clear, measured, with 1–2 second pauses after key terms. Include learning objectives at the start and 3 review questions at the end. Where appropriate, briefly connect clinical principles to a biblical view of marriage, family and personhood without replacing clinical rigor."

## 1ST SEMESTER

### MFT 701 – Fundamentals of Marriage and Family Therapy
**Best styles:** #17 Concept Map + #13 Timeline + #12 Analogy
**Prompt:** "Open with a full concept map of the MFT profession (history, core assumptions, major schools, professional identity, scope of practice). Zoom into each node, narrate it, and zoom back out. Use a timeline for the field's evolution from 1950s Palo Alto to today, and explain 'systems thinking' using the analogy of a mobile hanging from a ceiling — move one piece and all others shift."
**Why:** First course — students need the big picture before the parts.

### MFT 702 – Theories of Family Therapy
**Best styles:** #8 Compare & Contrast + #22 Day-in-the-Life
**Prompt:** "Present Bowenian, Structural, Strategic, Experiential, Narrative and Solution-Focused therapy in a split-screen comparison across 5 criteria: view of the problem, role of therapist, key techniques, goal of therapy, and typical first session. Then follow one fictional family through a 'first session' under three different models, narrating how each therapist would think and act."
**Why:** Theories blur together unless contrasted side-by-side and seen in action.

### MFT 703 – Human Development Across the Lifespan
**Best styles:** #13 Timeline + #32 Zoom In/Zoom Out
**Prompt:** "Build an animated lifespan timeline from infancy to late adulthood. At each stage, narrate Erikson's psychosocial task, attachment implications, and what a family therapist should watch for. Zoom from individual development out to how a family must adapt when a member enters each stage."
**Why:** Development is inherently chronological, and MFT needs the individual↔family link.

### MFT 704 – Ethical and Legal Issues in Therapy
**Best styles:** #27 Decision Tree + #23 Spot the Mistake
**Prompt:** "Present ethical decision-making as an animated flowchart (identify issue → consult AAMFT Code → consider law → consult supervisor → document → act). Then present 5 short case vignettes, each containing one ethical error — dual relationships, confidentiality in couples work, duty to warn, informed consent with minors, record keeping. Pause 5 seconds for students to spot it, then reveal."
**Why:** Ethics is learned through judgment under ambiguity, not memorized rules.

### MFT 705 – Research Methods in Marriage and Family Therapy
**Best styles:** #7 Chart-First + #21 Conclusion-First
**Prompt:** "Start by stating one published MFT research finding. Work backwards to show how it was produced: research question, design (qualitative vs quantitative vs mixed), sampling, measures, analysis, limitations. Build a single results chart progressively as each element is explained."
**Why:** Research feels abstract until students see a real finding reverse-engineered.

### MFT 706 – Clinical Practice and Techniques
**Best styles:** #10 Step-by-Step + #30 Role-Play
**Prompt:** "Demonstrate 6 core techniques — joining, circular questioning, reframing, enactment, genogram construction, scaling questions. For each: numbered steps on screen, a poor role-play version with two voices, narrated critique, then the improved version."
**Why:** Techniques are motor skills — they must be seen done badly and well.

### MFT 707 – Assessment and Diagnosis in Family Therapy
**Best styles:** #24 Layered Reveal + #3 Problem–Solution
**Prompt:** "Present one intake case. Reveal assessment layers one at a time: presenting problem → genogram (3 generations) → family structure map → interaction patterns → individual DSM-5-TR considerations → systemic formulation. Narrate what each layer adds that the previous one missed."
**Why:** Shows assessment as building a picture, not filling in a form.

### MFT 708 – Cultural Competence in Therapy
**Best styles:** #11 Myth-Busting + #33 Debate + #37 Thought Experiment
**Prompt:** "Open with 3 myths ('culture-blind is fair', 'one framework fits all families', 'religion is outside therapy's scope'). Dismantle each with evidence. Then a thought experiment: 'Imagine you are the only person in the room whose family structure is considered abnormal.' Close with the ADDRESSING model and the African/Nigerian extended-family context as a worked example."
**Why:** Cultural competence requires confronting assumptions, not acquiring facts.

## 2ND SEMESTER

### MFT 709 – Group Therapy Techniques
**Best styles:** #16 Two-Voice Dialogue + #25 Checklist
**Prompt:** "Script as a dialogue between a new therapist and a group-work supervisor covering Yalom's therapeutic factors, stages of group development, screening, and managing difficult members. End with a 'Running Your First Group' checklist."
**Why:** Group dynamics are best understood conversationally — it models the format itself.

### MFT 710 – Couples Therapy
**Best styles:** #30 Role-Play + #8 Compare & Contrast + #34 Before-and-After
**Prompt:** "Compare Gottman Method, EFT (Johnson), and Imago across their view of distress and key interventions. Then show one couple's conflict conversation 'before', narrate the Four Horsemen and attachment cycle visible in it, and replay it 'after' intervention with annotations."
**Why:** Couples work lives in observable interaction patterns — students must see them change.

### MFT 711 – Child and Adolescent Therapy
**Best styles:** #22 Day-in-the-Life + #31 Difficulty Ladder
**Prompt:** "Follow a child therapist through a day: a 6-year-old (play-based session), a 12-year-old (family session on school refusal), a 16-year-old (individual + parents on autonomy conflict). Rank the cases from Level 1 to 3 and narrate what developmental adaptation each level requires."
**Why:** Developmental stage changes everything; a day-in-the-life makes that concrete.

### MFT 712 – Crisis Intervention Strategies
**Best styles:** #27 Decision Tree + #40 Mission Briefing
**Prompt:** "Frame as a mission briefing: 'A family calls at 9 pm — the father has threatened suicide.' Present the intel: risk assessment (Columbia scale), safety planning, de-escalation, when to involve emergency services, documentation. Show as a decision tree, then issue the challenge with success criteria."
**Why:** Crisis skills must be rapid and procedural; urgency in the format aids recall.

### MFT 713 – Substance Abuse and Family Dynamics
**Best styles:** #5 Whiteboard + #12 Analogy + #9 Case Study
**Prompt:** "Whiteboard-draw the addiction cycle within a family system: roles (enabler, hero, scapegoat, lost child, mascot), homeostasis, and relapse triggers. Use the analogy of a family 'organizing around a missing chair'. Follow one case through Stages of Change and a family-involved treatment plan."
**Why:** The systemic nature of addiction is spatial — drawing it reveals the loops.

### MFT 714 – Trauma-Informed Care
**Best styles:** #37 Thought Experiment + #24 Layered Reveal + #35 Accessibility-First
**Prompt:** "Begin with a thought experiment on how the body remembers danger. Layer in the trauma response (window of tolerance, fight/flight/freeze/fawn), ACEs research, and intergenerational transmission. Narrate slowly, describe every visual aloud, include a content note at the start, and end with the 6 SAMHSA principles applied to a family session."
**Why:** Trauma content must be paced gently and modeled with the same care it teaches.

### MFT 715 – Family Systems Theory
**Best styles:** #12 Analogy + #24 Layered Reveal + #18 Demonstration
**Prompt:** "Explain systems concepts — homeostasis, feedback loops, boundaries, triangulation, differentiation — using a thermostat and an ecosystem as analogies. Build a family map layer by layer, then run a 'simulation': change one member's behavior and narrate the predicted ripple effects before revealing them."
**Why:** Systems thinking is counterintuitive; predict-then-observe makes it click.

## 3RD SEMESTER

### MFT 716 – Communication Skills in Therapy
**Best styles:** #30 Role-Play + #23 Spot the Mistake + #36 Guided Notes
**Prompt:** "Demonstrate active listening, reflection of feeling, immediacy, confrontation and silence through two-voice role-plays. Include 5 clips with a deliberate communication error for students to catch. Provide fill-in-the-blank notes for each micro-skill definition."
**Why:** Micro-skills need repetition and error recognition.

### MFT 717 – Psychopathology in Families
**Best styles:** #8 Compare & Contrast + #32 Zoom In/Out
**Prompt:** "For depression, anxiety, bipolar, personality disorders and psychosis: zoom in to the individual DSM-5-TR picture, then out to the family's typical accommodation patterns, burden, and expressed emotion. Contrast the 'medical model' and 'systemic model' views of each."
**Why:** MFTs must hold both individual diagnosis and systemic context at once.

### MFT 718 – Intimacy and Relationship Dynamics
**Best styles:** #2 Storytelling + #11 Myth-Busting
**Prompt:** "Tell the story of one couple from dating to 15 years married. Use their journey to teach attachment styles, Sternberg's triangle, the pursue–withdraw cycle, differentiation (Schnarch), and sexual intimacy as communication. Bust 3 myths along the way ('conflict means incompatibility', 'passion should never fade', 'good couples don't need help'). Optional: connect covenant love to the clinical concept of secure attachment."
**Why:** Intimacy is narrative — a story carries nuance that definitions can't.

### MFT 719 – Family Life Cycle and Transitions
**Best styles:** #13 Timeline + #22 Day-in-the-Life
**Prompt:** "Animate the Carter & McGoldrick family life cycle stages. At each transition (leaving home, marriage, young children, adolescents, launching, later life) narrate the required second-order change and common presenting problems. Include divorce/remarriage and the multigenerational African family as variant cycles."
**Why:** Transitions are where families get stuck — the timeline shows exactly where.

### MFT 720 – Supervision and Consultation in Therapy
**Best styles:** #16 Two-Voice Dialogue + #25 Checklist + #27 Decision Tree
**Prompt:** "Script a live supervision session between supervisor and supervisee covering a stuck case. Pause to narrate supervision models (developmental, systemic, isomorphism), parallel process, and evaluation. End with a checklist for preparing for supervision and a decision tree for 'when to consult vs refer'."
**Why:** Supervision is a relationship — hearing one modeled is the fastest teacher.

### MFT 721 – Research Methodology
**Best styles:** #10 Step-by-Step + #39 Live-Calculation + #34 Before-and-After
**Prompt:** "Walk through designing a thesis study step-by-step: problem statement → literature review → hypotheses → design → IRB/ethics → instruments → analysis plan. Show a weak draft research question 'before' and refine it live 'after'. Demonstrate one basic analysis on screen (e.g., a t-test or thematic coding) from scratch."
**Why:** This course directly feeds MFT 770 — students need a buildable process.

### MFT 770 – Master Thesis
**Best styles:** #40 Mission Briefing + #25 Checklist + #38 Synthesis
**Prompt:** "Frame the thesis as a mission with milestones and deadlines. Provide a chapter-by-chapter checklist (Introduction, Literature Review, Methodology, Results, Discussion), common examiner criticisms, and a synthesis map showing how courses 701–721 feed into the thesis."
**Why:** Thesis anxiety is mostly structural uncertainty — a mission plan removes it.

## ELECTIVES

### MFT 722 – Professional Development and Self-Care
**Best styles:** #20 Application-Focused + #28 Peer-Teaching
**Prompt:** "Informal peer-voice lecture on burnout, compassion fatigue, vicarious trauma, licensure pathways and continuing education. After each section, pose 'how would this look in your week?' and end with a personal self-care and career plan template. Optional: integrate Sabbath rhythm as a rest principle."
**Why:** Self-care content only works if it becomes personal action.

### MFT 723 – Special Populations in Therapy
**Best styles:** #9 Case Study + #33 Debate
**Prompt:** "Present 5 brief case studies: military families, families with a disabled member, blended families, families facing chronic illness, and immigrant/diaspora families. For each, narrate context, unique systemic pressures, adapted interventions. Include one 'debate' segment on a contested practice issue."
**Why:** Breadth of populations demands case-based variety.

### MFT 724 – Family Law and Advocacy
**Best styles:** #27 Decision Tree + #26 Primary Source
**Prompt:** "Read excerpts from an actual custody evaluation standard and a mandatory-reporting statute on screen. Build decision trees for mandated reporting, subpoenas and court testimony, and custody-related roles (therapist vs evaluator). Note differences between US jurisdictions and Nigerian family law where relevant."
**Why:** Legal material must be anchored to real texts and clear procedures.

### MFT 725 – Integrative Approaches in Therapy
**Best styles:** #17 Concept Map + #3 Problem–Solution
**Prompt:** "Map common-factors research, technical eclecticism, theoretical integration, and assimilative integration. Then take one complex case and narrate building an integrative treatment plan, explaining why each component was chosen and what was rejected. Include faith-integrated therapy as one model with its ethical safeguards."
**Why:** Integration is about principled choice — students must see the reasoning.

### MFT 726 – Neurobiology of Relationships
**Best styles:** #12 Analogy + #24 Layered Reveal + #5 Whiteboard
**Prompt:** "Whiteboard-draw the brain's social circuitry layer by layer: limbic system, vagal tone (Polyvagal theory), mirror neurons, oxytocin and cortisol, interpersonal neurobiology (Siegel). Use the analogy of 'two nervous systems in conversation'. Connect each to a couples or parenting intervention."
**Why:** Neuroscience intimidates therapy students — drawing and analogies demystify it.

### MFT 727 – Play Therapy Techniques
**Best styles:** #18 Demonstration + #10 Step-by-Step + #15 Documentary
**Prompt:** "Documentary-style introduction to why children communicate through play (Axline, Landreth). Then demonstrate 5 techniques step-by-step: sand tray, puppet interviews, feelings thermometer, filial therapy coaching, and therapeutic storytelling. Narrate the child's likely experience during each."
**Why:** Play therapy is visual and experiential — it must be shown, not described.

## Suggested Production Pattern for the Whole Programme

| Element | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Length per lecture | 12–18 min (or 3 × 5-min micro-lessons, style #19) |
| Opening | Learning objectives + one hook (myth, story, or case) |
| Body | Course-specific style stack above |
| Close | 3 review questions + application prompt (#20) |
| Voice | One consistent "lead lecturer" voice; a second voice only for role-plays and dialogues |
