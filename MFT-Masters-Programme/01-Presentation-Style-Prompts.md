# 40 Presentation-Style Prompts for Narrated Lecture Videos

Each prompt can be pasted into a slide/video generator or an AI scripting tool. Replace `[topic]` with the subject matter.

---

## Part 1 — Prompts 1–20

### 1. Classic Academic Lecture
**Prompt:** "Create a 10-minute lecture on [topic] structured as: learning objectives → core concepts → worked examples → summary → review questions. Narration should be measured, formal, and clearly signpost each section."
**Why it's important:** Mirrors how students are used to being taught; builds a predictable mental map.
**Best at:** University courses, certification prep, foundational theory.

### 2. Storytelling / Narrative Arc
**Prompt:** "Teach [topic] as a story with a protagonist facing a problem. Introduce tension, reveal the concept as the solution, and close with the resolution. Use a warm, conversational narration tone."
**Why it's important:** Stories are remembered far longer than bullet points; emotion anchors memory.
**Best at:** History, case studies, ethics, business lessons, onboarding.

### 3. Problem–Solution Walkthrough
**Prompt:** "Open with a concrete real-world problem involving [topic]. Spend 70% of the video solving it step-by-step on screen, narrating each decision and why alternatives were rejected."
**Why it's important:** Shows thinking, not just answers — learners see *how* experts reason.
**Best at:** Math, engineering, programming, troubleshooting.

### 4. Socratic / Question-Driven
**Prompt:** "Structure the lecture on [topic] entirely around 6 escalating questions. Pose each question, pause for 3 seconds, then narrate the reasoning toward the answer before raising the next question."
**Why it's important:** Activates curiosity and retrieval practice instead of passive listening.
**Best at:** Philosophy, law, critical thinking, science concepts with common misconceptions.

### 5. Whiteboard / Hand-Drawn Explainer
**Prompt:** "Produce a whiteboard-animation style lecture on [topic]. Visuals appear to be drawn in real time as the narrator speaks; keep text minimal and let diagrams carry the explanation."
**Why it's important:** Synchronizing drawing with speech reduces cognitive load (dual-coding).
**Best at:** Processes, systems, biology, economics flows, anything with cause-and-effect.

### 6. Slide-Minimalist (One Idea Per Slide)
**Prompt:** "Create a lecture on [topic] where every slide contains only one image or one phrase (max 6 words). Narration carries all detail. Transition slides every 20–30 seconds."
**Why it's important:** Prevents learners from reading ahead and tuning out the narrator.
**Best at:** Conceptual topics, TED-style talks, leadership and soft-skills training.

### 7. Data-Driven / Chart-First
**Prompt:** "Teach [topic] by building up a single chart progressively. Start with an empty axis, add one data series at a time, and narrate what each addition reveals. End with the full insight."
**Why it's important:** Progressive disclosure makes complex data approachable.
**Best at:** Statistics, economics, public health, business analytics, research findings.

### 8. Compare & Contrast
**Prompt:** "Present [concept A] versus [concept B] using a split-screen layout. For each of 5 criteria, narrate how each side performs, then conclude with when to choose which."
**Why it's important:** Learners understand things better by their boundaries and differences.
**Best at:** Technology choices, theories, frameworks, historical movements.

### 9. Case Study Deep Dive
**Prompt:** "Build a lecture around one real case: [organization/event]. Sections: context → what happened → analysis using [framework] → lessons → how to apply them. Narrate like a documentary."
**Why it's important:** Grounds abstract principles in reality and consequences.
**Best at:** Business, medicine, law, engineering failures, policy.

### 10. Step-by-Step Tutorial / Screencast
**Prompt:** "Create a screen-recorded tutorial on [task]. Number each step on screen, narrate what to click/type and *why*, and show the expected result after each step. Include a 'common mistakes' section."
**Why it's important:** Reduces frustration; learners can pause and follow along exactly.
**Best at:** Software, tools, lab procedures, any skill with a defined sequence.

### 11. Myth-Busting / Misconception-First
**Prompt:** "Start the lecture on [topic] by stating 3 widely-believed myths. Narrate why each seems plausible, then dismantle it with evidence. End with the corrected mental model."
**Why it's important:** Directly confronting wrong beliefs produces durable conceptual change.
**Best at:** Science, nutrition, finance, health, history.

### 12. Analogy & Metaphor Driven
**Prompt:** "Explain [complex topic] using one extended everyday analogy ([e.g., a city, a kitchen, a postal system]). Map every key component to the analogy visually and narrate the mapping explicitly."
**Why it's important:** Bridges unknown to known; the fastest route to intuition.
**Best at:** Computer science, physics, biology, abstract systems for beginners.

### 13. Timeline / Chronological
**Prompt:** "Present [topic] along an animated horizontal timeline. Advance through 6–8 milestones, narrating cause, event, and consequence for each. Zoom in on the two most pivotal moments."
**Why it's important:** Sequence and causality become visible; context is never lost.
**Best at:** History, evolution of technology, project retrospectives, scientific discovery.

### 14. Interactive Quiz-Embedded
**Prompt:** "Create a lecture on [topic] with a short quiz question every 3 minutes. Display the question, pause narration 5 seconds, reveal the answer with explanation, then continue."
**Why it's important:** Spaced retrieval during the video dramatically boosts retention.
**Best at:** Exam preparation, compliance training, language learning, memorization-heavy subjects.

### 15. Documentary / Cinematic
**Prompt:** "Produce a documentary-style lecture on [topic] with full-screen imagery, ambient music under the narration, and short on-screen captions. Narration should be authoritative yet evocative."
**Why it's important:** High production feel keeps attention and raises perceived credibility.
**Best at:** Geography, environment, culture, art history, inspirational course intros.

### 16. Expert Interview / Two-Voice Dialogue
**Prompt:** "Script the lecture on [topic] as a dialogue between a curious learner and an expert. The learner asks the questions a beginner would; the expert answers clearly. Use two distinct narration voices."
**Why it's important:** Conversational format feels less lecture-y and anticipates learner questions.
**Best at:** Introductions to any field, podcasts-to-video, professional development.

### 17. Concept Map / Big-Picture First
**Prompt:** "Open with a full concept map of [topic]. Then zoom into each node one at a time, narrate it, and zoom back out to show how it connects. End on the complete map again."
**Why it's important:** Learners always know where they are in the subject; relationships stay visible.
**Best at:** Dense subjects with many interlinked parts — anatomy, law, curriculum overviews.

### 18. Demonstration / Live Experiment
**Prompt:** "Teach [principle] through a demonstration or simulation shown on screen. Narrate a prediction first, run the demo, then explain the result and what would change under different conditions."
**Why it's important:** Predict-observe-explain creates genuine 'aha' moments.
**Best at:** Physics, chemistry, economics simulations, UX/behavioral experiments.

### 19. Micro-Lesson Series (Bite-Sized)
**Prompt:** "Break [topic] into 5 standalone 2–3 minute videos. Each opens with the single takeaway, explains it, shows one example, and ends with a one-line recap. Keep visuals and narration pace brisk."
**Why it's important:** Matches modern attention spans and mobile learning habits; easy to revise.
**Best at:** Corporate training, social media education, flipped classrooms, revision material.

### 20. Reflective / Application-Focused Close
**Prompt:** "Deliver [topic] with 60% content and 40% application. After each concept, pose a 'how would you use this in your situation?' prompt on screen with a brief narrated example, and end with a personal action plan template."
**Why it's important:** Transfers learning into behavior — the real goal of most lectures.
**Best at:** Professional skills, coaching, health education, personal finance, teacher training.

## Part 2 — Prompts 21–40

### 21. Inverted / Conclusion-First
**Prompt:** "Open the lecture on [topic] by stating the final conclusion in one sentence. Then work backwards, narrating the chain of evidence and reasoning that leads to it. Close by restating the conclusion with the full context now in place."
**Why it's important:** Learners know the destination, so every detail has a purpose instead of feeling random.
**Best at:** Research findings, executive briefings, policy explanations, exam-focused review.

### 22. Day-in-the-Life / Scenario Simulation
**Prompt:** "Teach [skill/topic] by following a fictional practitioner through a typical day. Each task they encounter introduces one concept. Narrate in present tense ('She opens the dashboard and notices…')."
**Why it's important:** Shows how knowledge is used in context, not just what it is.
**Best at:** Vocational training, nursing, hospitality, customer service, software roles.

### 23. Error Analysis / "Spot the Mistake"
**Prompt:** "Present 5 worked examples on [topic], each containing one deliberate error. Pause narration for 5 seconds after each so viewers can find the mistake, then reveal and explain why it's wrong and how to fix it."
**Why it's important:** Analyzing errors builds deeper understanding than only seeing correct solutions.
**Best at:** Mathematics, grammar, coding, accounting, medical dosage calculations.

### 24. Build-Up Diagram (Layered Reveal)
**Prompt:** "Explain [system] by revealing one layer of a diagram at a time — start with the simplest core, then add complexity in 5 stages. Narrate what each new layer adds and why the previous layer wasn't enough."
**Why it's important:** Prevents overwhelm; complexity is earned gradually.
**Best at:** Network architecture, cell biology, organizational structures, supply chains.

### 25. Checklist / Framework Walkthrough
**Prompt:** "Teach [framework or method] as a numbered checklist. For each item, show the checkbox, narrate its purpose, give one example of doing it well and one of skipping it. End with the full completed checklist as a downloadable recap."
**Why it's important:** Gives learners a reusable tool they can apply immediately.
**Best at:** Project management, safety procedures, quality control, writing processes.

### 26. Historical Reenactment / Primary Source
**Prompt:** "Teach [historical topic] by narrating directly from primary sources — letters, speeches, records. Show the source document on screen while reading excerpts aloud in character, then step out to narrate analysis in a neutral voice."
**Why it's important:** Builds source-literacy and emotional connection to the past.
**Best at:** History, literature, law (landmark rulings), political science.

### 27. Flowchart / Decision Tree
**Prompt:** "Present [decision-heavy topic] as an animated flowchart. Narrate each decision node, follow one branch to its outcome, then return and follow the alternatives. End with the full tree visible."
**Why it's important:** Makes conditional logic explicit and shows all paths at once.
**Best at:** Clinical diagnosis, legal procedure, troubleshooting, eligibility rules, algorithms.

### 28. Peer-Teaching / "Explain It to a Friend"
**Prompt:** "Script the lecture on [topic] as if a student who just learned it is explaining it to a classmate — informal language, occasional self-corrections, and simple sketches. Keep it under 6 minutes."
**Why it's important:** Lower intimidation; learners feel 'if they got it, I can too.'
**Best at:** High-school and first-year content, peer tutoring programs, study groups.

### 29. Visual Essay / Infographic Motion
**Prompt:** "Create a lecture on [topic] styled as an animated infographic. Use icons, large numbers, and short labels. Narration explains each statistic and connects it to the next with a clear transition."
**Why it's important:** Numbers become memorable when paired with strong visual anchors.
**Best at:** Social sciences, public awareness campaigns, market research, sustainability.

### 30. Role-Play / Dialogue Scenarios
**Prompt:** "Teach [communication skill] through 3 short role-play scenes with two voices. Show the poor version first, narrate what went wrong, then replay the improved version with annotations on screen."
**Why it's important:** Demonstrates behavior, not just principles — essential for interpersonal skills.
**Best at:** Negotiation, counseling, sales, patient communication, conflict resolution.

### 31. Progressive Difficulty Ladder
**Prompt:** "Teach [skill] through 5 problems ranked Level 1 to Level 5. Narrate each solution fully, and explicitly state what new idea each level requires beyond the previous one."
**Why it's important:** Learners can self-locate their ability and see a clear path to mastery.
**Best at:** Mathematics, programming, chess, language grammar, music theory.

### 32. Zoom In / Zoom Out (Scale Journey)
**Prompt:** "Explain [topic] by moving across scales — start at the largest view, zoom step-by-step to the smallest component, then zoom back out. Narrate how each scale depends on the one below it."
**Why it's important:** Connects micro and macro understanding that is often taught separately.
**Best at:** Biology (ecosystem → cell), economics (global → household), geography, astronomy.

### 33. Debate Format / Two Sides Presented
**Prompt:** "Present [controversial or multi-perspective topic] as a structured debate. Narrate the strongest case for Side A, then Side B, then a neutral synthesis highlighting where evidence is solid versus uncertain."
**Why it's important:** Models intellectual honesty and teaches learners to weigh evidence.
**Best at:** Ethics, policy, economics, scientific controversies, literature interpretation.

### 34. Before-and-After Transformation
**Prompt:** "Open with a 'before' state (messy code, weak essay, poor design, unhealthy process). Narrate each improvement step while the screen transforms it. Close with a side-by-side before/after comparison."
**Why it's important:** Visible transformation makes the value of the lesson undeniable.
**Best at:** Writing, design, coding refactors, process improvement, fitness/health.

### 35. Accessibility-First / Universal Design
**Prompt:** "Create a lecture on [topic] designed for accessibility: narration describes every visual aloud, high-contrast slides, captions throughout, no information conveyed by color alone, and a slower pace with clear enunciation."
**Why it's important:** Reaches learners with visual, hearing, or cognitive differences — and improves clarity for everyone.
**Best at:** Public education, government and compliance training, inclusive classrooms, audio-only consumption.

### 36. Guided Note-Taking (Fill-in-the-Blank)
**Prompt:** "Produce a lecture on [topic] with on-screen notes that have key terms blanked out. Narrate the full content; reveal each blank 3 seconds after it's spoken so viewers can write it first."
**Why it's important:** Active writing during the lecture roughly doubles retention compared to passive viewing.
**Best at:** Secondary school, vocabulary-heavy subjects, anatomy, legal definitions.

### 37. Thought Experiment
**Prompt:** "Introduce [abstract concept] through a thought experiment ('Imagine you are…'). Narrate the scenario slowly with minimal visuals, let the paradox or insight emerge, then connect it to the formal concept."
**Why it's important:** Engages imagination and reasoning before terminology, producing genuine understanding.
**Best at:** Physics, philosophy, economics, ethics, probability.

### 38. Recap & Synthesis (End-of-Module)
**Prompt:** "Create a 7-minute synthesis video connecting [list of 5–6 previous lessons]. Show each lesson as a node, narrate how they relate, resolve any apparent contradictions, and end with the 'one big idea' that unifies them."
**Why it's important:** Consolidates fragmented lessons into a coherent whole — where deep learning actually happens.
**Best at:** End of units, exam review, capstone courses, onboarding wrap-ups.

### 39. Live-Coding / Live-Calculation Style
**Prompt:** "Teach [topic] by writing the code/calculation live on screen from a blank editor. Narrate your thinking out loud including hesitations, test runs, and bug fixes. Don't hide mistakes — explain them."
**Why it's important:** Shows the real, non-linear process of problem-solving that polished tutorials hide.
**Best at:** Programming, data analysis, spreadsheet modeling, engineering calculations.

### 40. Challenge-Based / Mission Briefing
**Prompt:** "Frame the lecture on [topic] as a mission briefing. Present a challenge the learner must complete, narrate the knowledge and tools they'll need as 'intel', and end by issuing the challenge with success criteria."
**Why it's important:** Purpose-driven learning increases motivation and completion rates.
**Best at:** Gamified courses, youth education, hackathon prep, skills bootcamps, corporate upskilling.

## Tips for Pairing with Voice Narration
- **Match tone to style:** formal for #1/#7, warm for #2/#16, brisk for #19.
- **Script for the ear, not the eye:** short sentences, spoken contractions, explicit transitions.
- **Leave breathing room:** 1–2 second pauses after key points help comprehension and make synthetic voices sound natural.
- **Mix styles:** a strong lecture often opens with #11 or #2, teaches with #3 or #5, and closes with #14 or #20.
