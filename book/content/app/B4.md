# Appendix B4: Complex Portfolio Cases and Model Planning Prompts

## Case 17: The Revolving Door

**Presentation.** Mara, sixteen, has had four counsellors in eighteen months due to staff turnover, placement change, and service age thresholds. She attends once, tells a detailed trauma history quickly, then does not return. Records describe “poor engagement”.

**Formulation prompts.** Consider attachment, epistemic trust, service discontinuity, assessment fatigue, and the possibility that rapid disclosure is a test or a learned attempt to secure help before disappearance. The system may be training the pattern it condemns.

**Plan prompts.** Offer continuity guarantees that are actually deliverable, a named backup practitioner, a concise shared formulation so Mara does not restart from zero, control over pacing, and an explicit repair route after missed sessions. Measure trust and participation, not only symptoms.

## Case 18: The Examination Countdown

**Presentation.** Eli, seventeen, studies at a high-performing school. He has panic attacks, insomnia, and thoughts that failure would “prove” he has wasted his parents' sacrifices. His parents immigrated and work long hours. They say they never pressure him.

**Formulation prompts.** Examine explicit and implicit expectations, perfectionism, family sacrifice narratives, school comparison systems, sleep, caffeine, identity, and possible depression or obsessive-compulsive symptoms. Avoid reducing parental love to pressure or Eli's ambition to pathology.

**Plan prompts.** Medical and risk assessment; sleep protection; examination planning; graded exposure to imperfect work; family conversation about love, worth, and disappointment; school advocacy on mock examination feedback; career exploration that widens the definition of a viable future.

## Case 19: The Sibling Who Became Security

**Presentation.** Owen, ten, follows his seven-year-old autistic brother everywhere at school and intervenes in peer conflict. Staff praise him. Owen has stomach pain and refuses clubs because “he might need me”.

**Formulation prompts.** Sibling subsystem, parentification, school reliance on a child for supervision, anxiety, disability support gaps, and family stress.

**Plan prompts.** Increase adult supervision and sibling support; give Owen protected peer time; create a plan for his brother that does not depend on Owen; family session validating both children's needs. Do not solve an institutional staffing issue through a child's vigilance.

## Case 20: The Last Ten Minutes

**Presentation.** In nearly every counselling session, Hana, fifteen, becomes distressed in the final ten minutes and asks to stay longer. The counsellor repeatedly overruns, then feels resentful. Hana says sessions are the only place adults do not rush her.

**Formulation prompts.** Attachment, endings, time boundaries, possible testing, session pacing, unprocessed material introduced too late, and practitioner countertransference.

**Plan prompts.** Structure sessions with a closing phase, summarise earlier, agree what can be carried safely, plan post-session support, and address the pattern collaboratively. Reliability includes ending on time with warmth, not only extending in crisis.

## Portfolio Marking Guidance

High-quality portfolio work:

- distinguishes facts, reports, inferences, and unknowns;
- centres the young person's voice without treating it as the only evidence;
- integrates development, culture, family, school, and material context;
- names risk and protective processes dynamically;
- sequences interventions rather than collecting techniques;
- assigns institutional responsibilities to institutions;
- protects confidentiality and obtains meaningful assent;
- identifies practitioner limits and referral points;
- states outcomes that matter in ordinary life;
- revises formulation when evidence changes.

Weak work diagnoses from a paragraph, stereotypes culture, blames parents or the young person, lists brand-name therapies, ignores safeguarding, or proposes individual coping for structural harm.
