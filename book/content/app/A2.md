# Appendix A2: Confidentiality, Information Sharing, and Records

## A2.1 Confidentiality Agreement for Counselling with Minors

This model should be adapted to law and service policy. It is designed to be read aloud and discussed, not merely signed.

### What stays within counselling

Session conversation, questionnaires, artwork, digital material created for therapy, and the counsellor's process reflections are treated as confidential clinical information. Access is limited to authorised professionals with a legitimate role under policy.

### What may be shared routinely with agreement

Practical information may include attendance, appointment changes, broad progress, agreed goals, safety plans, and recommendations for home or school. The young person should know the wording or substance whenever possible.

### What may or must be shared without agreement

Information may be shared where there is serious risk of harm, suspected abuse or exploitation, a legal duty, a court order, or another lawful safeguarding basis. Only relevant information should be shared, with the minimum necessary audience. Unless doing so would increase danger or obstruct protection, the young person will be told what is being shared, with whom, and why.

## A2.8 Retention and Destruction Note

Services must not promise destruction at a date contrary to law, safeguarding requirements, insurance, or organisational retention schedules. When records reach review, record the schedule applied, reviewer, decision, method of secure destruction where authorised, and any legal hold. Digital deletion should include relevant backups and vendor systems as far as contractually and technically possible, with limitations documented.
