# Appendix D3: Glossary of Terms (R–Z)

:::keyterms
- **Radical behaviourism** — Skinner's philosophy treating private events as behaviour subject to functional analysis.
- **Rapport** — Harmonious connection supporting communication.
- **Rational emotive behaviour therapy (REBT)** — Ellis's approach identifying and disputing rigid beliefs and building healthier philosophies.
- **Reactivity** — Change in behaviour because it is being observed or measured.
- **Re-authoring** — Narrative development of preferred accounts using unique outcomes and values.
- **Reciprocal determinism** — Bandura's bidirectional influence among person, behaviour, and environment.
- **Recovery** — Personal process of living a meaningful life with or without continuing symptoms; also clinical symptom improvement depending on context.
- **Reflection (listening)** — Response conveying understanding of a speaker's meaning or feeling.
- **Reflective functioning** — Capacity to reflect on mental states in self and others.
- **Reformulation** — Revised formulation based on new evidence or response to intervention.
- **Refractory problem** — Problem persisting despite apparently appropriate intervention; a prompt to reformulate, not blame.
- **Regression** — Return to earlier developmental modes under stress.
- **Reinforcement** — Consequence increasing future behaviour.
- **Reinforcement schedule** — Pattern governing when consequences follow behaviour.
- **Relapse** — Return of symptoms or behaviour after improvement.
- **Relapse prevention** — Planning for warning signs, high-risk contexts, coping, and help seeking.
- **Relational frame** — Context of relationships through which behaviour and meaning are understood.
- **Reliability** — Consistency of measurement or judgement.
- **Reparation** — Making amends or repairing relational harm.
- **Resilience** — Dynamic process of adaptation under adversity supported by individual and environmental systems.
- **Resistance** — Ambivalence, disagreement, avoidance, or interaction pattern opposing change; not simply a client defect.
- **Resourcefulness** — Ability to identify and use internal and external resources.
- **Response cost** — Removal of a valued stimulus following behaviour.
- **Response prevention** — Blocking compulsive or avoidance responses during exposure-based work.
- **Restorative practice** — Voluntary processes addressing harm, accountability, and repair.
- **Risk assessment** — Structured identification and formulation of possible harms, contexts, and safeguards.
- **Risk factor** — Factor associated with increased probability of adverse outcome.
- **Role dispute** — IPT problem area involving non-reciprocal expectations in a relationship.
- **Role play** — Enactment of a situation for learning, rehearsal, or perspective taking.
- **Role transition** — IPT problem area involving change from an old role to a new one.
- **Routine outcome monitoring** — See outcome monitoring.
- **Rumination** — Repetitive, passive focus on distress and its causes or consequences.
- **Rupture (alliance)** — Strain or break in therapeutic collaboration, goals, tasks, or bond.
- **Safeguarding** — Protecting children and vulnerable people from abuse, neglect, exploitation, and harm.
- **Safety behaviour** — Action intended to prevent feared catastrophe that may maintain anxiety by blocking new learning.
- **Safety plan** — Collaborative plan identifying warning signs, coping, supports, professional help, and means safety.
- **Scaling question** — Solution-focused rating of present position, hope, confidence, or progress.
- **Schema** — Organised pattern of knowledge, belief, affect, and response.
- **Schema therapy** — Integrative therapy addressing early maladaptive schemas, coping styles, and modes.
- **School belonging** — Sense of acceptance, respect, safety, and participation at school.
- **School refusal** — Difficulty attending school associated with distress; requires functional and contextual assessment.
- **Screening** — Brief process identifying who may need fuller assessment.
- **Secondary emotion** — Emotion arising in response to a primary emotion, such as shame about anger.
- **Secure attachment** — Pattern in which care is sufficiently available and the child can use the caregiver as safe haven and secure base.
- **Secure base** — Relationship support from which exploration and learning occur.
- **Self-concept** — Organised perceptions and evaluations of oneself.
- **Self-determination** — Experience of autonomy, competence, and relatedness supporting motivation.
- **Self-efficacy** — Task- and context-specific belief in capability.
- **Self-esteem** — Global evaluation of self-worth.
- **Self-harm** — Intentional self-injury or poisoning irrespective of motive; intent and risk require assessment.
- **Self-monitoring** — Observing and recording one's behaviour, mood, thoughts, or physiology.
- **Self-regulation** — Managing attention, emotion, impulses, and behaviour toward goals and safety.
- **Self-soothing** — Strategies used to reduce distress; developmental capacity grows through co-regulation.
- **Sensorimotor** — Relating to sensation and movement.
- **Separation anxiety** — Excessive anxiety concerning separation from attachment figures or home.
- **Session rating scale** — Brief client feedback measure concerning session fit or alliance.
- **Setting event** — Broader contextual condition altering behaviour–consequence relations.
- **Sexual orientation** — Pattern of romantic, emotional, and/or sexual attraction.
- **Shame** — Painful self-conscious emotion involving perceived defectiveness or exposure.
- **Shaping** — Reinforcing successive approximations toward a target behaviour.
- **Sibling subsystem** — Family subsystem comprising siblings.
- **Significant other** — Important person in a client's relational world.
- **Single-case design** — Systematic evaluation of intervention effects with an individual over time.
- **Skill acquisition** — Learning through instruction, modelling, rehearsal, feedback, and practice.
- **Sleep hygiene** — Habits and environments supporting sleep; clinical sleep problems may require more than hygiene advice.
- **Social anxiety** — Fear of scrutiny, embarrassment, or negative evaluation in social or performance situations.
- **Social capital** — Resources available through relationships and networks.
- **Social clock** — Cultural expectations about timing of life transitions.
- **Social determinants of health** — Conditions in which people live, learn, work, and age that shape health.
- **Social learning** — Learning through observation, imitation, and vicarious consequences.
- **Social support** — Emotional, practical, informational, and companionship resources from relationships.
- **Solution-focused brief therapy** — Future-oriented approach using exceptions, scaling, goals, and client resources.
- **Somatic** — Relating to bodily experience.
- **Splitting** — Experiencing self or others in extreme all-good/all-bad terms; also team division into opposing views.
- **Stabilisation** — Phase of establishing safety, regulation, resources, and capacity before demanding processing.
- **Standardised measure** — Instrument administered and scored consistently with normative or criterion references.
- **Stepped care** — Matching intervention intensity to need and response, increasing or decreasing over time.
- **Stigma** — Social devaluation, stereotypes, prejudice, and discrimination linked to an identity or condition.
- **Stimulus** — Event or condition that may elicit or set the occasion for behaviour.
- **Strengths-based practice** — Assessment and intervention centred on capacities, resources, values, and hopes without denying harm.
- **Stress** — Response to demands perceived or experienced as exceeding resources.
- **Structural family therapy** — Minuchin's approach to subsystems, boundaries, hierarchy, and organisation.
- **Subjective units of distress (SUDS)** — Self-rating of distress, often 0–10 or 0–100.
- **Subsystem** — Part of a family system with its own roles and boundaries.
- **Suicidal ideation** — Thoughts about suicide, ranging from passive wishes to specific intent and plans; always requires contextual assessment.
- **Suicide risk formulation** — Dynamic explanation of factors, triggers, protective processes, and actions concerning suicide risk.
- **Supervision** — Professional process supporting competence, ethics, reflection, and accountability.
- **Support network** — People and organisations providing practical or emotional help.
- **Sustain talk** — Language favouring status quo or opposing a particular change.
- **Symptom** — Subjective experience or observable sign associated with distress or disorder.
- **Systemic** — Concerning interacting relationships, contexts, and feedback loops.
- **Teleological mode** — Mentalizing failure in which only visible action counts as proof of intention or care.
- **Teletherapy** — Counselling delivered through telecommunications.
- **Termination** — Ending phase or event in counselling.
- **Theory** — Organised set of concepts explaining phenomena and guiding observation or intervention.
- **Therapeutic alliance** — See alliance.
- **Therapeutic frame** — Boundaries of time, place, roles, fees where relevant, confidentiality, and contact.
- **Thought record** — Structured record of situation, thoughts, feelings, evidence, alternatives, and outcome.
- **Time-out** — Brief removal from reinforcing activity as part of a safe, planned behaviour strategy; not isolation or humiliation.
- **Titration** — Working in small doses to remain within tolerance.
- **Token economy** — System using tokens exchangeable for backup reinforcers.
- **Tolerance (window of)** — Arousal range in which a person can process, learn, and relate without overwhelm or shutdown.
- **Transactional analysis** — Berne's theory of ego states, transactions, games, scripts, and strokes.
- **Transference** — Relational expectations from earlier relationships activated in therapy.
- **Transition** — Change in role, setting, relationship, development, or status.
- **Trauma** — Experience or pattern that overwhelms coping and may produce lasting effects; definitions vary by framework.
- **Trauma-focused CBT** — Trauma-specific cognitive-behavioural treatment involving psychoeducation, regulation, narration/processing, and caregiver work.
- **Trauma-informed care** — Service approach recognising trauma prevalence and prioritising safety, trust, collaboration, empowerment, and cultural responsiveness.
- **Treatment fidelity** — See fidelity.
- **Treatment plan** — Document specifying formulation-based goals, interventions, responsibilities, measures, and review.
- **Triangle** — Three-person relational pattern in which tension between two is managed through a third.
- **Trigger** — Cue associated with emotional, bodily, behavioural, or memory responses.
- **Trust** — Expectation of reliability, honesty, benevolence, and respect.
- **Unconditional positive regard** — Rogers' non-possessive acceptance of the person.
- **Unconscious** — Mental processes outside current awareness.
- **Unique outcome** — Narrative term for events inconsistent with a problem-saturated story.
- **Universal prevention** — Intervention delivered to a whole population regardless of individual risk.
- **Validation** — Communicating that experience is understandable; not necessarily agreement with behaviour.
- **Validity** — Extent to which evidence supports interpretation and use of a measure or assessment.
- **Values** — Chosen directions or qualities guiding action.
- **Vicarious learning** — Learning by observing consequences experienced by others.
- **Vicarious trauma** — Practitioner changes resulting from empathic engagement with trauma material.
- **Visual schedule** — Pictorial or written sequence supporting predictability and independence.
- **Volition** — Capacity and experience of choosing or willing action.
- **Vulnerability** — Susceptibility to harm influenced by biology, development, relationships, and context.
- **Wellbeing** — Broad state of health, functioning, connection, meaning, and life satisfaction.
- **Withdrawal (social)** — Reduced participation in relationships or activities.
- **Withdrawal (substance)** — Physiological and psychological effects after reducing or stopping a substance.
- **Working alliance** — See alliance.
- **Working memory** — System for temporarily holding and manipulating information.
- **Wraparound care** — Coordinated, family-driven planning across services and natural supports.
- **Youth voice** — Meaningful participation of young people in decisions affecting them.
- **Zone of proximal development** — Vygotsky's range between independent performance and performance possible with support.
:::
