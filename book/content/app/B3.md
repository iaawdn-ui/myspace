# Appendix B3: Case Studies in Family, Identity, and Systems

## Case 11: The Eldest Daughter's Timetable

Ruth, seventeen, organises medication, translates appointments, cooks for siblings, and supports her mother after night shifts. School calls her exceptional. She has stopped applying for courses away from home and says she has no preferences “as long as everyone is okay”.

Students should examine young caring, gendered expectations, family resources, cultural values, identity foreclosure, and service responsibility. A successful plan reduces extracted resilience rather than praising it. Include respite, young-carer assessment, adult interpreting, school flexibility, and supported exploration of futures. Ask what Ruth might want if wanting did not feel disloyal.

## Case 12: The Name on the Register

Alex, fifteen, uses a chosen name with friends and a different name at home. A school system automatically prints the legal name on a classroom list projected to the class. Alex leaves school and refuses to return until “adults stop making it a thing”.

Students should address administrative safety, confidentiality, bullying, family disclosure planning, staff training, record systems, and the difference between affirmation and forced visibility. The first intervention may be technical: change the list. Identity support fails when databases keep outing the student.

## Case 13: The House with Two Alarms

Fatima, thirteen, lives with her mother and stepfather. She becomes aggressive after school but is calm in lessons. Her mother says Fatima saves all anger for home. Teachers suspect the school report is exaggerated.

Students should avoid assuming home is either safe or unsafe based on one setting. Explore after-school restraint collapse, sensory load, sleep, family conflict, possible neurodevelopmental needs, and the mother's experience. Design observation across settings and a decompression routine. Assess safeguarding directly and separately.

## Case 14: The Boy Who Coached Himself

Diego, twelve, is an talented footballer who panics before matches. He watches elite athletes online and compares his body and performance. His coach praises “mental toughness” and benches players who show distress.

Students should integrate anxiety, body image, social learning, sport culture, parent and coach behaviour, and performance systems. Intervention may include graded exposure, coping models, coach consultation, sleep and nutrition referral, and redefining courage as flexible participation rather than suppression.

## Case 15: A New Country, an Old Profession

Samira's mother was a pharmacist before migration and now cleans at night. Samira interprets for her and feels both proud and trapped. Her mother grieves status loss but tells school she is “fine”.

Students should explore intergenerational acculturation, credential recognition, grief, language brokering, class change, racism, and family pride. The counsellor should not turn structural loss into a communication exercise alone. Community, employment, language, and credential-navigation resources are part of care.

## Case 16: The Student Council President

Nia, seventeen, leads campaigns, supports friends, and achieves top grades. She presents after fainting and admitting she eats very little before school events. She says asking for help would make her “a fraud”.

Students should arrange medical assessment, explore perfectionism, identity, social reward for selflessness, family and school expectations, and possible eating disorder pathways. The plan must not rely solely on insight. High public functioning can conceal high private risk.

## Integrative Questions for Cases 11–16

1. Which adult system benefits from the young person's current coping?
2. Which strength is being overused until it becomes a burden?
3. What would change if the institution altered one routine?
4. What identity story is available to the young person besides patient, carer, problem, or high achiever?
5. Which professional is coordinating, and does the young person know?
6. What cultural assumptions did your first formulation make?
7. What outcome would the young person call success?
8. What would make you stop, refer, or escalate?
