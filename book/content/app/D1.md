# Appendix D: Glossary of Terms (A–G)

This glossary prioritises working definitions for students. It does not replace diagnostic manuals, statutes, or specialist texts.

:::keyterms
- **Abstinence violation effect** — A lapse after a period of abstinence interpreted as total failure, increasing risk of full relapse.
- **Accommodation (family)** — Family changes that reduce a young person's immediate distress but may maintain avoidance or symptoms.
- **Acting out** — Expressing conflict or feeling through action rather than words or reflection.
- **Active listening** — Listening that communicates attention through posture, minimal encouragers, reflection, and summary.
- **Activity scheduling** — Planning valued, mastery, social, and restorative activities as part of treatment.
- **Acute risk** — Immediate or near-term danger requiring urgent action.
- **Adaptive behaviour** — Behaviour that helps a person manage demands in a context, even if it has longer-term costs.
- **Adolescence** — Developmental period involving puberty, identity, autonomy, peer belonging, and transition toward adult roles; boundaries vary.
- **Adverse childhood experiences (ACEs)** — Potentially traumatic events or conditions in childhood associated at population level with later health risks.
- **Affect** — Observable expression of emotion.
- **Affect regulation** — Processes by which emotional arousal is noticed, tolerated, modulated, and expressed.
- **Affirmation** — In motivational interviewing, specific recognition of strengths, values, effort, or autonomy.
- **Agency** — Capacity to initiate action and influence one's life within real constraints.
- **Agenda mapping** — Collaboratively identifying and prioritising topics for change.
- **Agoraphobia** — Anxiety and avoidance concerning places or situations from which escape may be difficult or help unavailable during panic-like symptoms.
- **Alliance** — Collaborative therapeutic relationship involving bond, goals, and tasks.
- **Ambivalence** — Simultaneous reasons for and against a change or course of action.
- **Amplification** — Gestalt or reflective technique of exploring an experience more fully; must be used without flooding.
- **Anhedonia** — Reduced ability to experience pleasure or interest.
- **Antecedent** — Event or condition occurring before a behaviour in functional analysis.
- **Anxiety** — Anticipatory fear response involving cognition, physiology, and avoidance or preparation.
- **Applied behaviour analysis (ABA)** — Systematic application of learning principles to socially significant behaviour, including functional assessment.
- **Approach behaviour** — Movement toward a feared, valued, or needed stimulus or goal.
- **Arousal** — Physiological and psychological activation.
- **Assertiveness** — Communicating needs and boundaries while respecting others.
- **Assessment** — Systematic gathering and interpretation of information for decisions.
- **Assent** — A young person's affirmative agreement to participate, distinct from legal consent.
- **Assimilation (cognitive)** — Interpreting new experience through existing schemas; in cultural contexts it may have different meanings and should be defined locally.
- **Attachment** — Enduring relational system involving proximity, safe haven, secure base, and expectations of care.
- **Attachment figure** — Person used for comfort, protection, and exploration support.
- **Attending** — Practitioner behaviours that communicate presence and attention.
- **Attention-maintained behaviour** — Behaviour maintained partly by social responses it produces.
- **Attribution** — Explanation assigned to an event or behaviour.
- **Automatic thought** — Rapid evaluative thought arising in a situation.
- **Autonomic nervous system** — Nervous system divisions involved in arousal, digestion, cardiovascular and threat responses.
- **Autonomy** — Self-direction consistent with development and safety.
- **Avoidance** — Behaviour that prevents contact with feared, painful, or demanding stimuli and is often negatively reinforced.
- **Baseline** — Measurement before intervention used for comparison.
- **Behaviour** — Observable action; some traditions also include private events defined functionally.
- **Behavioural activation** — Treatment increasing rewarding, mastery, social, and value-based activity to interrupt depressive withdrawal.
- **Behavioural experiment** — Planned test of a belief or prediction through action.
- **Belonging** — Experience of acceptance, recognition, and participation in a group or setting.
- **Bibliotherapy** — Guided use of reading materials as an adjunct to care.
- **Bilateral stimulation** — Alternating sensory stimulation used in EMDR protocols.
- **Biopsychosocial model** — Framework considering biological, psychological, and social factors together.
- **Body scan** — Attention moved systematically through bodily sensations; requires consent and trauma sensitivity.
- **Boundaries** — Limits and expectations governing roles, contact, privacy, and responsibility.
- **Brief therapy** — Time-conscious treatment organised around focal goals and efficient change.
- **Broaching** — Inviting discussion of identity, culture, difference, and power.
- **Bullying** — Repeated harmful behaviour involving power imbalance, including online forms.
- **Burnout** — Occupational exhaustion, cynicism, and reduced efficacy associated with chronic work stress.
- **Case conceptualisation** — Collaborative formulation explaining development and maintenance of difficulties and guiding treatment.
- **Case management** — Coordination of services, referrals, and practical supports.
- **Catharsis** — Emotional release; not by itself sufficient evidence of therapeutic change.
- **Change talk** — Client language expressing desire, ability, reasons, need, commitment, activation, or steps toward change.
- **Chosen family** — Self-selected network providing kinship-like support.
- **Circadian rhythm** — Approximately daily biological cycle affecting sleep, alertness, hormones, and mood.
- **Classical conditioning** — Learning through association between stimuli.
- **Clinical formulation** — See case conceptualisation.
- **Clinical significance** — Practical importance of a change beyond statistical significance.
- **Co-regulation** — Regulation of arousal through relationship with another person.
- **Cognition** — Mental processes including attention, memory, interpretation, belief, and problem-solving.
- **Cognitive defusion** — ACT process of noticing thoughts as thoughts rather than literal commands or facts.
- **Cognitive distortion** — Biased or inaccurate pattern of interpretation; best used descriptively rather than as blame.
- **Cognitive restructuring** — Identifying, testing, and revising unhelpful appraisals or beliefs.
- **Cognitive triad** — Beck's negative view of self, world/experience, and future in depression.
- **Cohesion** — In groups or families, sense of belonging and commitment.
- **Collaborative empiricism** — CBT stance in which client and practitioner test ideas together.
- **Collective efficacy** — Shared belief that a group can organise and act effectively.
- **Common factors** — Therapeutic elements shared across models, including alliance, empathy, expectancy, goals, and feedback.
- **Communication analysis** — Detailed reconstruction of an interpersonal exchange.
- **Comorbidity** — Presence of more than one condition or difficulty at the same time.
- **Compassion** — Concern for suffering with motivation to help; in MI, prioritising client welfare.
- **Competence** — Knowledge, skill, judgement, and ethical capacity to perform a professional task.
- **Conditioned response** — Learned response to a previously neutral stimulus after association.
- **Confidentiality** — Ethical and legal duty to protect private information within stated limits.
- **Conflict (interpersonal)** — Dispute arising from incompatible goals, expectations, needs, or interpretations.
- **Congruence** — Practitioner genuineness and consistency between experience and communication.
- **Consent** — Informed, voluntary agreement by a person with legal authority and capacity for the decision.
- **Consequence** — Event following behaviour that may alter its future likelihood.
- **Containment** — Relational process by which overwhelming affect is received, tolerated, and returned in manageable form.
- **Contingency** — Relationship between behaviour and its consequences.
- **Contingency management** — Planned alteration of reinforcement and consequences in a setting.
- **Coping** — Cognitive, behavioural, emotional, relational, or spiritual-neutral strategies used to manage demands; may be helpful or costly.
- **Core belief/schema** — Deeply held general belief about self, others, or world.
- **Corrective experience** — New relational or behavioural experience that updates expectations.
- **Countertransference** — Practitioner emotional and relational responses in treatment, requiring reflection.
- **Crisis** — Situation in which demands exceed current coping or safety resources.
- **Crisis plan** — Agreed steps, contacts, and supports for periods of elevated risk.
- **Cultural formulation** — Assessment of cultural identity, explanations, context, and clinician-client differences.
- **Cultural humility** — Lifelong self-critique, openness, and accountability regarding culture and power.
- **Culture** — Shared and contested patterns of meaning, practice, identity, language, values, and institutions.
- **Defence mechanism** — Psychological strategy reducing awareness of painful affect or conflict.
- **De-escalation** — Actions that reduce arousal, threat, and likelihood of harm.
- **Deliberate self-harm** — Self-injury or self-poisoning, regardless of suicidal intent; local terminology should be used carefully.
- **Denial** — Defence involving refusal to acknowledge aspects of reality.
- **Dependence** — Reliance on a person, substance, or behaviour; developmental dependence is normal in childhood.
- **Desensitisation** — Reduced emotional response after repeated or graded contact.
- **Developmental cascade** — Effects spreading across domains and developmental periods.
- **Developmental trauma** — Impact of chronic, interpersonal adversity during development; not a stand-alone diagnosis in major manuals.
- **Diagnosis** — Classification of symptoms and patterns for communication, planning, and eligibility.
- **Dialectics** — Holding apparently opposing truths together, such as acceptance and change.
- **Differentiation of self** — Capacity to maintain self-definition while remaining emotionally connected.
- **Digital safety** — Practices reducing online exploitation, bullying, privacy loss, and harmful contact.
- **Disenfranchised grief** — Grief not socially recognised or supported.
- **Disputation** — REBT process of questioning rigid or irrational beliefs.
- **Dissociation** — Disruption in consciousness, memory, identity, perception, or sense of self, often associated with overwhelm.
- **Distress tolerance** — Capacity to endure painful states without making them worse.
- **Dorsal vagal language** — Polyvagal-informed term for shutdown-related states; theoretical claims should be taught cautiously.
- **Double bind** — Contradictory communications with limited ability to comment or escape.
- **Dropout** — Ending treatment before agreed completion; meaning should be investigated rather than assumed.
- **Dual-systems model** — Adolescent model contrasting reward sensitivity with developing cognitive control.
- **Dyad** — Two-person relationship or session format.
- **Ecological systems theory** — Bronfenbrenner's model of nested developmental contexts.
- **Efficacy (self-)** — Belief in capability for a specific task in a specific context.
- **Ego states** — In transactional analysis, Parent, Adult, and Child patterns of feeling, thinking, and behaving.
- **Emotion coaching** — Helping a person notice, name, validate, regulate, and act on emotion.
- **Emotion-focused therapy** — Treatment working with primary and secondary emotions and unmet needs.
- **Emotional cutoff** — Distancing from family relationships to manage unresolved attachment intensity.
- **Empathy** — Understanding and communicating another person's experience from their frame of reference.
- **Empowerment** — Increasing agency, voice, resources, and control.
- **Enactment** — Relational pattern expressed in action, including in therapy.
- **Engagement** — Process of establishing participation and working relationship.
- **Epistemic trust** — Openness to learning from another as a trustworthy source of social knowledge.
- **Escape-maintained behaviour** — Behaviour maintained by removing or postponing demands or aversive conditions.
- **Ethics** — Principles and standards governing professional conduct.
- **Evidence-based practice** — Integration of best research evidence, clinical expertise, client preferences, and context.
- **Exception** — In solution-focused work, a time when the problem is absent, weaker, or handled differently.
- **Exposure** — Planned contact with feared stimuli or memories under conditions that permit new learning.
- **Externalisation** — Narrative practice separating person from problem so the problem's influence can be examined.
- **Extinction** — Reduction of behaviour when reinforcement is withheld; may involve an initial burst.
- **Family projection process** — Transmission of parental anxiety or unresolved issues onto a child.
- **Family systems** — Patterns of interaction, structure, boundaries, and feedback in families.
- **Feedback-informed care** — Routine use of client outcome and alliance feedback to adjust treatment.
- **Feminist counselling** — Practice analysing gender, power, social context, and empowerment.
- **Fidelity** — Degree to which an intervention is delivered as intended.
- **Fight-or-flight** — Mobilising threat responses preparing confrontation or escape.
- **Flashback** — Intrusive re-experiencing of traumatic material as if occurring now.
- **Flooding** — Intense exposure approach; generally requires caution and is not first-line for most youth presentations.
- **Formulation** — See case conceptualisation.
- **Freeze** — Threat response involving immobility or shutdown.
- **Functional analysis** — Assessment of antecedents, behaviours, consequences, and maintaining functions.
- **Functional behaviour assessment** — Educational and clinical assessment of behaviour purpose and context.
- **Gender dysphoria** — Clinically significant distress associated with incongruence between experienced gender and assigned sex, as defined in diagnostic systems.
- **Gender expression** — External communication of gender through appearance, behaviour, voice, and roles.
- **Gender identity** — Internal sense of one's gender.
- **Generalisation** — Transfer of learning or behaviour change across settings, people, and time.
- **Genogram** — Family diagram showing relationships, patterns, events, and generations.
- **Goal** — Desired outcome guiding treatment; should be meaningful and reviewable.
- **Grief** — Response to loss, including emotional, bodily, cognitive, social, and behavioural dimensions.
- **Grounding** — Techniques orienting attention to present sensory reality to manage arousal or dissociation.
- **Group cohesion** — Members' sense of belonging, trust, and commitment in a group.
