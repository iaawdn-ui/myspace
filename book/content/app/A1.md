# Appendix A: Consent, Assent, and Records Forms

## A1. Purpose and Use of These Forms

These model forms support counselling with children and adolescents in educational, community, clinic, and private-practice settings. They are teaching templates, not legal advice. Before use, each service must adapt them to current local law, professional regulation, safeguarding policy, record-retention rules, insurance requirements, and organisational governance. Where a young person's safety or legal rights are involved, qualified local advice should be obtained.

Consent is not a signature obtained once and forgotten. It is an ongoing process of explanation, understanding, choice, review, and withdrawal. For minors, legal consent may rest with a parent, guardian, court, or the young person, depending on age, capacity, jurisdiction, and the nature of the service. Assent is the young person's affirmative agreement to participate even where another person provides legal consent. Dissent—refusal, withdrawal, distress, silence, or persistent non-attendance—must be noticed and explored rather than overridden merely because paperwork has been signed.

A form should never promise what the service cannot deliver. Do not promise absolute confidentiality, immediate crisis response outside operating hours, permanent record destruction contrary to retention law, or access to a particular practitioner if staffing cannot guarantee it.

## A2. Core Elements of a Consent Record

A complete counselling consent record should identify:

- the legal name of the young person and the name they wish to be used in sessions and communications;
- date of birth or age range where required for service eligibility;
- parent, guardian, or legal decision-maker details where applicable;
- the service name, practitioner name and role, supervisor or accountable lead where relevant;
- purpose and likely nature of counselling;
- session length, frequency, location or remote platform;
- fees, cancellation rules, and funding source where applicable;
- benefits, alternatives, and foreseeable limitations;
- confidentiality and its limits;
- record creation, access, storage, retention, and sharing;
- communication methods and response times;
- safeguarding and emergency arrangements;
- complaint and advocacy routes;
- the right to ask questions, pause, withdraw, or seek another service;
- signatures, dates, interpreter details, and any adjustments provided.

Where consent is obtained verbally because of literacy, disability, remote access, or urgent circumstances, record who explained the information, what was explained, how understanding was checked, who agreed, and any witnesses or interpreters used under policy.

## A4. Young Person's Assent Statement

My counsellor is ____________________. I understand that counselling is a time to talk, think, practise skills, or use other agreed ways of working on things that matter to me.

I understand that:

- I can say what I think the problem is and what I want to change.
- I can ask what will happen with information I share.
- What I say is private unless the counsellor needs to share information to keep me or someone else safe, or the law/policy says information must be shared.
- The counsellor may need to speak to adults about practical arrangements or safety, but we will discuss what will be said whenever possible.
- I can ask for a pause, say I do not want to answer, or ask for a different way of working.
- I can ask who keeps notes, where they are kept, and how to complain or get support from another adult or advocate.
- If sessions are online or by phone, we will agree where I will be, who can hear, what happens if the connection fails, and how I can contact help in an emergency.
- I can change my mind about counselling. If I stop coming, the counsellor or service may contact me or my parent/carer in the way we agreed, especially if they are worried about safety.

My preferred name in sessions is: ____________________

Adults may use this name in these settings: ____________________

Adults should not use this name or share information about counselling in these settings without discussing it with me first: ____________________

The best safe way to contact me is: ____________________

I agree to take part in counselling at this time.

Young person's signature or mark: ____________________ Date: __________

## A5. Capacity and Communication Checklist

Before relying on consent or assent, consider and record:

- Can the young person understand the purpose and nature of counselling in accessible language?
- Can they retain the main choices long enough to decide?
- Can they weigh obvious benefits, burdens, and alternatives?
- Can they communicate a preference by speech, sign, writing, device, behaviour, or trusted support?
- Are pain, medication, fatigue, intoxication, distress, language, disability, or another person’s presence affecting apparent agreement?
- Has an interpreter, advocate, or communication aid been offered where needed?
- Has the young person had an opportunity to speak without a parent, teacher, partner, or peer present?
- Are there signs of coercion by family, school, partner, peers, or professionals?
- What adjustments were made, and what remains uncertain?

Capacity is decision-specific and may fluctuate. A young person may be able to choose session activities but need support for record-sharing decisions, medical referrals, or complex family meetings.

## A6. Review and Withdrawal Record

Consent and assent should be reviewed when:

- goals or methods materially change;
- a new person joins treatment;
- family or school information-sharing changes;
- teletherapy begins or ends;
- recording, testing, or digital tools are introduced;
- risk or safeguarding status changes;
- the young person reaches a relevant legal age or changes placement;
- treatment extends beyond the period originally explained;
- the young person expresses doubt, dissent, or misunderstanding.

## A7. Common Errors to Avoid

Do not use a parent's signature to silence an adolescent's dissent. Do not obtain consent for “counselling” and then introduce trauma processing, group participation, recording, testing, or school reporting without renewed explanation. Do not state that records are confidential if school administrators, insurers, digital platforms, or courts may access them under policy or law. Do not guarantee that a young person can withdraw from safeguarding action. Do not make counselling a condition of avoiding punishment without explaining the mandated frame and preserving meaningful choices inside it.

The ethical test is simple: if the young person, parent, or future reader of the record saw exactly what had been explained and agreed, would they recognise the service that followed?
