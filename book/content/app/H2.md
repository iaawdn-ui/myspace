# Appendix H2: Crisis Response, Safety Planning, and Service Directory Worksheets

## H7. Immediate Crisis Response Sequence

This sequence supports professional judgement and local emergency procedures; it does not replace them.

1. **Ensure immediate safety.** Reduce access to means where possible, involve responsible adults or emergency services, and do not leave a young person alone if policy and risk require continuous support.
2. **Use a calm, direct approach.** Ask clearly about thoughts, intent, plans, means, intoxication, injuries, and immediate supports.
3. **Obtain medical help** for injury, ingestion, overdose, severe agitation, psychosis, or physical deterioration.
4. **Contact safeguarding and emergency supports** according to law and policy.
5. **Limit information sharing** to what is needed for protection, while being honest with the young person where safe.
6. **Document** times, observations, statements, actions, consultations, and handovers.
7. **Arrange follow-up** before the young person leaves the practitioner's responsibility. A referral sent is not a risk managed.
8. **Support staff and family**, review the response, and update the plan.

Do not ask a young person to promise never to self-harm as a substitute for assessment and safety planning. “No-suicide contracts” are not evidence-based protections.

## H8. Post-Crisis Review

Within an appropriate period after a crisis, review:

- What happened, in sequence?
- What warning signs were present or missed?
- What helped and what increased distress?
- Were confidentiality, dignity, culture, disability, and language handled well?
- Were family, school, health, and safeguarding communications timely?
- Was any restrictive intervention used, and was it lawful, proportionate, documented, and reviewed?
- What needs to change in the safety plan, environment, staffing, or referral route?
- Does the young person want a debrief, repair conversation, advocate, or complaint route?
- What support do involved staff need?

Post-crisis learning should improve systems without converting a frightened young person into a case study without consent.

## H12. Practitioner Crisis Pack Checklist

A service should maintain current, accessible information on:

- emergency and safeguarding contacts;
- designated safeguarding leads;
- local mental health crisis pathways;
- medical and sexual assault services;
- interpreter access;
- out-of-hours responsibilities;
- incident reporting;
- lone-working rules;
- remote-session emergency procedures;
- post-incident supervision and debrief;
- complaint and advocacy routes.

Practitioners should not carry the entire system in personal memory. Crisis competence is partly organisational design.


## Presentation-Specific Referral Pathways

Pathways must be localised. A referral should state the decision requested and continue support while waiting.

### Persistent school avoidance

Potential services include school leadership, attendance, health, educational psychology, mental health, family support. The referral should include attendance pattern, safety, curriculum access, health and sleep, family plan, plus the young person's goals, communication and access needs, relevant strengths, current supports, and what has already been tried. State urgency honestly and identify who will follow up if the referral is delayed or rejected. Avoid copying entire therapy notes; share the minimum necessary for the decision. Where the young person disagrees with referral, record their view and explain any duty to proceed. A pathway is not complete until receipt is confirmed and the young person knows what happens next.
