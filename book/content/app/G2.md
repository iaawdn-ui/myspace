# Appendix G2: Supervision Agendas, Peer Review, and Reflective Writing

## G7. Individual Supervision Agenda

1. Immediate safety, safeguarding, legal, or ethical issues.
2. Cases with deterioration, rupture, or uncertainty.
3. One case presented in depth.
4. Skills observation or transcript review.
5. Cultural, power, and practitioner-position reflection.
6. Service constraints and advocacy.
7. Learning actions and reading.
8. Practitioner wellbeing and boundaries.
9. Review of previous actions.

Supervision record: date, attendees, cases by identifier, decisions, rationale, actions, review dates. Store supervision records according to policy, separate from client notes where required.

## G8. Group Supervision Protocol

A 90-minute group might include:

- five minutes: urgent risk or ethical notices;
- twenty minutes: Case A presentation and clarifying questions;
- fifteen minutes: multiple theoretical readings;
- ten minutes: agreed actions;
- twenty minutes: Case B skills transcript or recording (with consent);
- fifteen minutes: systems and equity theme;
- five minutes: learning commitments.

Ground rules: confidentiality within lawful limits, no identifiable information beyond need, curiosity before advice, challenge ideas not people, no requirement to disclose personal trauma, and explicit attention to whose perspective is missing.

## G10. Reflective Essay Structure

Use this structure for assessed reflection without disclosing unnecessary personal or client material.

**Situation.** Briefly describe the event using fictional or properly anonymised material.

**Observation.** Separate facts, feelings, bodily responses, and interpretations.

**Theory.** Apply at least two concepts from the course and identify where they conflict.

**Culture and power.** Examine identity, institutional position, language, and whose knowledge was privileged.

**Ethics.** Identify duties, consent, confidentiality, safeguarding, and boundaries.

**Learning.** State what changed in your understanding, with evidence.

**Action.** Specify one practice change, one learning activity, and one supervision question.

**Evaluation.** How will you know whether the change helped a future client?

## G12. Difficult Supervision Questions

- What am I avoiding asking this client or family?
- Which theory am I using to protect myself from uncertainty?
- Who would describe this case completely differently?
- What if the problem is the service arrangement, not the young person?
- Am I continuing because it helps, because ending feels like rejection, or because the system rewards activity?
- What would I do if this were my last session?
- What evidence would make me refer, escalate, or stop?
- Whose voice has not been heard directly?
- What is the smallest ethical action available today?
