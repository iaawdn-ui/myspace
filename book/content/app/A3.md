## A3.2 School Counselling Information Statement

School counselling is a supportive service. It is not a disciplinary interview, behaviour sanction, academic assessment, or investigation. The school counsellor may provide brief counselling, consultation to staff, family liaison with appropriate consent, group work, crisis support, and referral.

Students may be referred by staff, family, or themselves. Referral does not mean the student is in trouble. The counsellor will explain who made the referral and why in developmentally appropriate terms.

Session content is confidential within professional and legal limits. Teachers may receive practical recommendations or broad progress information where the student agrees or where policy permits. Teachers will not ordinarily receive transcripts or detailed disclosures. Safeguarding information will be passed to designated staff promptly.

## A3.3 Group Counselling Agreement

Group purpose: _______________________________________________________

Members are expected to attend on time, respect different experiences, avoid recording or screenshots, and not repeat other members' personal disclosures outside the group. The facilitator cannot guarantee that every member will maintain confidentiality; members should share only what they are prepared to have heard in a group.

Members may pass on an activity, ask for a pause, or speak to the facilitator individually. Threats, harassment, sexual or discriminatory comments, and pressure to disclose are not permitted. Safeguarding limits apply.

For online groups, members must join from a private-enough location, use the agreed name display, keep cameras or microphones as agreed, and not invite unapproved people into the session.

## A3.6 Consent Where Parents Are Separated or Legal Authority Is Disputed

Record, from reliable documentation where available:

- persons with parental responsibility or legal authority;
- court orders affecting contact, health, education, residence, travel, or information;
- who may consent to counselling and receive information;
- safeguarding restrictions on addresses, schools, photographs, or contact;
- who should receive appointment messages;
- any disagreement and legal advice or court direction obtained.

Do not rely solely on one adult's account where documents are available and conflict is known. Do not disclose a protected address or school. Where parents disagree about non-urgent counselling, follow local law and policy, consider the young person's views and best interests, and obtain legal or senior advice where necessary.

The young person should not be used as messenger between adults. Session arrangements should avoid placing them in loyalty conflicts.

## A3.8 Periodic Consent Review Prompts

At review, ask:

- Do you still understand why counselling is happening?
- What has been helpful, unhelpful, surprising, or uncomfortable?
- Is there anything you believed would stay private that has been shared?
- Has who you want involved changed?
- Has the safest contact method, name, pronoun, address, school, or platform changed?
- Do you want to continue, pause, change method, involve someone new, or end?
- Are there records or summaries you want explained or corrected?
- What should happen if you stop attending?

Document the answers in the young person's words where material. Consent is alive only if review can alter practice.
