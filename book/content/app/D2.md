# Appendix D2: Glossary of Terms (H–P)

:::keyterms
- **Habit reversal** — Behavioural procedure involving awareness, competing responses, and practice for repetitive behaviours.
- **Habituation** — Decreased response after repeated stimulation; not the only mechanism proposed for exposure.
- **Harm reduction** — Practical strategies reducing negative consequences of risky behaviour without requiring immediate abstinence as the only goal.
- **Hierarchy (exposure)** — Ordered list of feared situations from manageable to most difficult.
- **Hierarchy (family)** — Organisation of authority and responsibility across generations and subsystems.
- **Homeostasis** — System tendency to maintain stability, sometimes including symptom patterns.
- **Homework** — Between-session practice agreed in therapy.
- **Hyperarousal** — Excessive activation involving vigilance, startle, irritability, tension, or panic.
- **Hypoarousal** — Reduced activation involving numbness, shutdown, slowed response, or dissociation.
- **Hypermentalizing** — Overconfident, elaborate interpretation of mental states from limited evidence.
- **Hypomentalizing** — Limited reflection on mental states.
- **Identity** — Organised sense of self across time, roles, values, groups, body, and future.
- **Idiographic measure** — Measure tailored to an individual's goals or target behaviours.
- **Immediacy** — Practitioner use of here-and-now relationship experience.
- **Impulsivity** — Action with limited forethought; may reflect development, arousal, neurodivergence, substances, or context.
- **Incongruence** — Rogers' discrepancy between self-concept and experience.
- **Individuation** — Development of distinct identity and autonomy within relationships.
- **Informed consent** — Consent based on understandable information about nature, benefits, risks, alternatives, and rights.
- **Inhibitory learning** — Exposure account in which new safety learning competes with threat associations.
- **Insight** — Understanding of patterns or meanings; useful but not always sufficient for change.
- **Internal object** — Mental representation of self in relation to another, charged with affect.
- **Internal working model** — Expectations about self, others, and relationships derived from attachment experience.
- **Internalised stigma** — Applying negative social stereotypes to oneself.
- **Interpersonal inventory** — IPT assessment of important relationships and problem areas.
- **Interpersonal psychotherapy (IPT)** — Time-limited treatment targeting grief, disputes, transitions, and interpersonal deficits.
- **Intersectionality** — Framework analysing interacting identities and systems of power.
- **Intervention** — Intentional action intended to improve health, functioning, learning, or wellbeing.
- **Interview (motivational)** — See motivational interviewing.
- **Intimate partner violence** — Physical, sexual, psychological, economic, or coercive harm within intimate relationships; adolescent dating violence requires safeguarding.
- **Introjection** — Internalising another's attitudes or projections as part of self-experience.
- **Invalidation** — Communication that emotions or perceptions are wrong, excessive, or unacceptable.
- **Irrational belief (REBT)** — Rigid, illogical, or absolutist belief associated with disturbance in Ellis's model.
- **Joining** — Family therapy process of entering the family's style to build alliance and influence.
- **Joint session** — Meeting involving young person and family members or professionals together.
- **Learned helplessness** — Expectation that action cannot influence outcomes after uncontrollable aversive experience.
- **Learning theory** — Accounts of behaviour change through conditioning, observation, cognition, and environment.
- **Least restrictive intervention** — Effective option imposing the least limitation on rights and autonomy.
- **Limit setting** — Clear, proportionate boundaries for safety and therapeutic structure.
- **Listening (reflective)** — Responding that conveys understanding of content, feeling, meaning, or ambivalence.
- **Locus of control** — Beliefs about whether outcomes are mainly controlled by oneself or external forces.
- **Loss** — Loss of a person, role, place, capacity, relationship, status, or imagined future.
- **Maintenance** — Preservation of gains after active treatment.
- **Maladaptive** — Pattern that creates harm or impairment in context, even if originally protective.
- **Mandated treatment** — Treatment required by court, school, safeguarding, or another authority.
- **Marked mirroring** — Caregiver reflection that represents a child's feeling without simply reproducing it overwhelmingly.
- **Mastery experience** — Successful performance providing evidence of capability.
- **Meaning-making** — Process of constructing significance from experience.
- **Mechanism of change** — Process through which intervention produces outcome.
- **Medical model** — Framework understanding problems primarily as disorders requiring diagnosis and treatment.
- **Mental health literacy** — Knowledge and beliefs aiding recognition, management, and prevention of mental distress.
- **Mentalization** — Understanding behaviour in terms of mental states.
- **Mentalization-based treatment (MBT)** — Treatment restoring mentalizing under arousal, especially in relational difficulties.
- **Mesosystem** — Connections among a young person's immediate settings.
- **Metacognition** — Awareness and regulation of one's own thinking.
- **Metacommunication** — Communication about communication or relationship.
- **Microsystem** — Immediate environment in which a person participates.
- **Mindfulness** — Present-moment attention with openness and non-judgement; clinical adaptations vary.
- **Minority stress** — Stress from stigma, discrimination, vigilance, concealment, and internalised negativity.
- **Miracle question** — Solution-focused question imagining life after the problem is solved.
- **Modelling** — Learning or teaching through observation of behaviour and consequences.
- **Mood monitoring** — Tracking mood and related variables over time.
- **Motivational interviewing (MI)** — Collaborative conversation strengthening a person's own motivation and commitment to change.
- **Multisystemic therapy** — Intensive family- and community-based intervention for serious adolescent antisocial behaviour.
- **Narrative therapy** — Practice separating persons from problems and developing preferred stories and unique outcomes.
- **Negative reinforcement** — Removal of an aversive condition that increases a behaviour.
- **Neurodevelopmental conditions** — Conditions affecting brain development, including autism, ADHD, intellectual disability, communication and specific learning disorders.
- **Neuroplasticity** — Nervous system's capacity to change with experience.
- **Nominal group technique** — Structured method for generating and prioritising ideas.
- **Nomothetic measure** — Standardised measure enabling comparison with groups or norms.
- **Non-suicidal self-injury** — Deliberate injury without suicidal intent; intent can be mixed and must be assessed.
- **Not-knowing stance** — Curious recognition that mental states cannot be known with certainty.
- **Object relations** — Theories of internalised self–other relationship patterns.
- **Observational learning** — Learning by attending to, retaining, reproducing, and being motivated by modelled behaviour.
- **Obsessions** — Intrusive, unwanted thoughts, images, or urges.
- **Compulsions** — Repetitive behaviours or mental acts performed to reduce distress or prevent feared events.
- **Operant conditioning** — Learning through consequences of behaviour.
- **Operational definition** — Behaviour defined in observable, measurable terms.
- **Oppositional behaviour** — Argumentative, defiant, or hostile behaviour; description is not an explanation.
- **Orienting** — Sensory process of locating oneself in present environment.
- **Outcome expectation** — Belief about likely consequences of behaviour.
- **Outcome monitoring** — Repeated measurement of progress and feedback during care.
- **Overgeneralisation** — Drawing broad conclusions from limited events.
- **Panic attack** — Sudden surge of intense fear or discomfort with physical and cognitive symptoms.
- **Parentification** — Child assuming adult practical or emotional roles.
- **Parent training** — Structured support helping caregivers use effective, safe, relationship-supporting practices.
- **Partnership (MI)** — Collaborative working relationship rather than expert imposition.
- **Parts work** — Clinical language for internal subpersonalities or states; requires care not to literalise beyond client meaning and evidence.
- **Peer support** — Support from people with shared experience, in informal or organised forms.
- **Pendulation** — Moving attention between activation and resource in somatic work.
- **Perpetuating factors** — Processes maintaining a problem.
- **Person-centred therapy** — Humanistic approach based on empathy, congruence, and unconditional positive regard.
- **Personalisation** — Interpreting events as personally targeted without sufficient evidence.
- **Phobia** — Marked, persistent fear and avoidance of specific objects or situations causing impairment.
- **Physiological state** — Bodily arousal, fatigue, pain, hunger, illness, and hormonal or medication effects influencing behaviour.
- **Planned ignoring** — Deliberate withdrawal of attention from a behaviour while maintaining safety and reinforcing alternatives.
- **Positive reinforcement** — Addition of a consequence that increases behaviour.
- **Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD)** — Disorder following trauma involving intrusion, avoidance, altered cognition/mood, and arousal under diagnostic criteria.
- **Power differential** — Unequal authority, knowledge, age, institutional status, or resources in a relationship.
- **Precipitating factors** — Events triggering onset or escalation.
- **Predisposing factors** — Earlier vulnerabilities and contexts increasing likelihood of difficulty.
- **Pretend mode** — Mental-state talk disconnected from affect and lived experience.
- **Prevention** — Action reducing incidence, severity, or impact of problems.
- **Primary emotion** — Immediate adaptive emotional response to a need or event.
- **Privilege** — Unearned advantage linked to social position or system design.
- **Problem area (IPT)** — Focal category: grief, dispute, role transition, or interpersonal deficit.
- **Problem-solving therapy** — Structured method for defining problems, generating options, choosing, implementing, and reviewing.
- **Procedural learning** — Learning embodied in patterns of action and response rather than explicit knowledge.
- **Projection** — Attributing disowned feelings or motives to another.
- **Projective identification** — Interpersonal process in which disowned states are evoked or enacted in another.
- **Promotive factor** — Resource associated with positive outcomes across risk levels.
- **Protective factor** — Resource reducing probability or impact of adverse outcomes.
- **Protocol** — Specified sequence and procedures for an intervention.
- **Proximal goal** — Near-term goal supporting a larger distal goal.
- **Psychic equivalence** — Treating feelings or beliefs as external facts.
- **Psychodynamic** — Approaches emphasising unconscious processes, conflict, defence, development, and relational repetition.
- **Psychoeducation** — Structured information about a condition, development, treatment, or coping.
- **Psychological flexibility** — ACT capacity to remain present and act according to values despite difficult internal experiences.
- **Psychosis** — Conditions involving impaired reality testing such as delusions, hallucinations, or disorganised thinking; requires specialist assessment.
- **Punishment** — Consequence reducing behaviour; positive punishment adds aversive stimulus, negative punishment removes valued stimulus.
:::
