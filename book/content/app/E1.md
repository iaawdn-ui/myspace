# Appendix E: Annotated Bibliography (Foundations, Humanistic, Cognitive, and Behavioural Works)

This bibliography is a reading pathway, not a claim that every listed work is equally current or uncontested. Students should read primary texts alongside systematic reviews and critical literature.

## Foundations and Professional Practice

**Beck, J. S. (2020). *Cognitive Behavior Therapy: Basics and Beyond* (3rd ed.).** A clear clinical exposition of cognitive conceptualisation, session structure, and interventions. Best read as a treatment manual rather than a complete account of adolescent development.

**Carr, A. (2016). *The Handbook of Child and Adolescent Clinical Psychology* (3rd ed.).** A broad reference covering development, assessment, disorders, family contexts, and interventions. Useful for placing CAC. 701 theories in a wider clinical landscape.

**Corey, G. (2024). *Theory and Practice of Counseling and Psychotherapy* (11th ed.).** Accessible comparative introduction. Students should move from this survey to primary and evidence-focused sources.

**Johnstone, L., & Dallos, R. (Eds.). (2014). *Formulation in Psychology and Psychotherapy* (2nd ed.).** Cross-theoretical essays on formulation, power, narrative, and clinical judgement. Particularly valuable for Chapters 51–53.

**Kazdin, A. E. (2008). *Parent Management Training*.** A foundational account of behavioural parent training, treatment design, and evidence for disruptive behaviour.

**Norcross, J. C., & Goldfried, M. R. (Eds.). (2019). *Handbook of Psychotherapy Integration* (3rd ed.).** Comprehensive treatment of theoretical integration, eclecticism, common factors, and assimilative approaches. Use with Chapter 51.

**Prochaska, J. O., & Norcross, J. C. (2018). *Systems of Psychotherapy* (9th ed.).** Comparative theory text linking models to transtheoretical processes of change.

**Wampold, B. E., & Imel, Z. E. (2015). *The Great Psychotherapy Debate* (2nd ed.).** Major statement of common factors and contextual model. Read critically alongside disorder-specific treatment research.

**Weisz, J. R., & Kazdin, A. E. (Eds.). (2017). *Evidence-Based Psychotherapies for Children and Adolescents* (3rd ed.).** Essential evidence reference for youth treatments, moderators, implementation, and research limitations.

## Humanistic, Existential, Gestalt, and Narrative Works

**Cooper, M. (2008). *Essential Research Findings in Counselling and Psychotherapy*.** Helpful synthesis on relationship, client resources, and pluralistic practice.

**Epston, D., & White, M. (1992). *Experience, Contradiction, Narrative and Imagination*.** Selected papers showing narrative practice as conversation, documentation, and social action.

**Gendlin, E. T. (1978). *Focusing*.** Primary account of felt sense and experiential listening. Pair with contemporary evidence reviews rather than treating experiential report as outcome evidence.

**Mearns, D., Thorne, B., & McLeod, J. (2013). *Person-Centred Counselling in Action* (4th ed.).** Practical and relational account of Rogers' approach with attention to practitioner use of self.

**Perls, F., Hefferline, R., & Goodman, P. (1951). *Gestalt Therapy*.** Foundational but historically situated. Contemporary trauma and cultural critiques should inform use of experiments.

**Rogers, C. R. (1951). *Client-Centered Therapy*.** Primary source for the therapeutic conditions and client-directed change.

**Rogers, C. R. (1961). *On Becoming a Person*.** Accessible essays on self, relationships, education, and becoming. Useful for teaching but not a substitute for outcome research.

**White, M. (2007). *Maps of Narrative Practice*.** Mature exposition of externalising conversations, re-authoring, unique outcomes, and definitional ceremonies.

**White, M., & Epston, D. (1990). *Narrative Means to Therapeutic Ends*.** Foundational narrative text. Read with attention to power, discourse, and the social contexts of stories.

**Yalom, I. D. (1980). *Existential Psychotherapy*.** Classic organisation around death, freedom, isolation, and meaninglessness. Developmentally adapt before applying to younger adolescents.

## Cognitive and Third-Wave Works

**Beck, A. T. (1976). *Cognitive Therapy and the Emotional Disorders*.** Historical foundation of cognitive therapy and the cognitive model.

**Beck, A. T., Rush, A. J., Shaw, B. F., & Emery, G. (1979). *Cognitive Therapy of Depression*.** Primary treatment text whose session methods remain influential.

**Ellis, A. (1962). *Reason and Emotion in Psychotherapy*.** Foundational REBT statement. Students should compare its confrontational heritage with collaborative contemporary practice.

**Hayes, S. C., Strosahl, K. D., & Wilson, K. G. (2012). *Acceptance and Commitment Therapy* (2nd ed.).** Comprehensive ACT account including relational frame theory, psychological flexibility, values, and clinical methods.

**Kendall, P. C. (Ed.). (2012). *Child and Adolescent Therapy: Cognitive-Behavioral Procedures* (4th ed.).** Developmental CBT methods, anxiety programmes, family involvement, and school applications.

**Linehan, M. M. (2015). *DBT Skills Training Manual* (2nd ed.).** Core skills curriculum. Adolescent adaptations and family components should be studied before use.

**Miller, A. L., Rathus, J. H., & Linehan, M. M. (2007). *Dialectical Behavior Therapy with Suicidal Adolescents*.** Key adolescent DBT adaptation addressing self-harm, family involvement, and treatment structure.

**Segal, Z. V., Williams, J. M. G., & Teasdale, J. D. (2018). *Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy for Depression* (2nd ed.).** Main MBCT text, including relapse prevention and inquiry. Adapt cautiously for adolescents and trauma presentations.

**Young, J. E., Klosko, J. S., & Weishaar, M. E. (2003). *Schema Therapy*.** Primary guide to schemas, coping styles, and modes. Useful where chronic interpersonal patterns persist.

## Behavioural and Learning Works

**Bandura, A. (1977). *Social Learning Theory*.** Foundational statement on modelling and vicarious learning.

**Bandura, A. (1997). *Self-Efficacy: The Exercise of Control*.** Comprehensive treatment of efficacy beliefs across development, health, education, and collective action.

**Cooper, J. O., Heron, T. E., & Heward, W. L. (2020). *Applied Behavior Analysis* (3rd ed.).** Detailed ABA reference. Read with disability ethics, assent, and historical critiques.

**Ferster, C. B., & Skinner, B. F. (1957). *Schedules of Reinforcement*.** Primary experimental source, historically important though not a clinical manual.

**Iwata, B. A., et al. (1994).** Research programme on functional analysis of self-injurious behaviour. Important for understanding experimental functional analysis and its constraints.

**Kazdin, A. E. (2005). *Parent Management Training*.** Evidence and procedures for changing coercive cycles and building positive parenting practices.

**Maag, J. W. (2018). *Behavior Management: From Theoretical Implications to Practical Applications* (3rd ed.).** School-oriented behavioural practice with functional and ethical dimensions.

**Martell, C. R., Dimidjian, S., & Herman-Dunn, R. (2022). *Behavioral Activation for Depression* (2nd ed.).** Clear behavioural activation manual applicable with developmental adaptation.

**Skinner, B. F. (1953). *Science and Human Behavior*.** Core radical behaviourist account. Pair with contemporary critiques and cognitive, cultural, and relational developments.

**Wolpe, J. (1958). *Psychotherapy by Reciprocal Inhibition*.** Historical source for systematic desensitisation and behavioural treatment of fear.
