# Assessment Blueprint, Assignments, and Rubrics

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, the student and lecturer should be able to:

- Map CAC. 701 assessments to course outcomes and programme expectations.
- Design valid, reliable, fair, and feasible assignments for theory learning.
- Write transparent marking criteria and rubrics.
- Assess case conceptualisation, evidence appraisal, skills demonstration, reflection, and professional judgement.
- Provide feedback that students can use in subsequent work.
- Apply academic integrity, accessibility, moderation, and appeals principles.
- Use assessment data to improve teaching and demonstrate course quality.

## Chapter Overview

Assessment tells students what the course truly values. If lectures praise cultural humility, developmental reasoning, and client voice but examinations reward only memorised names and dates, students will memorise names and dates. The CAC. 701 blueprint therefore assesses theory as professional capability: seeing, formulating, choosing, justifying, practising, evaluating, and revising.

## 1. Blueprint Principles

A defensible blueprint states:

- course learning outcomes;
- programme and professional competencies addressed;
- assessment tasks and weighting;
- timing and formative preparation;
- criteria and standards;
- moderation and feedback arrangements;
- accessibility and integrity requirements.

Validity asks whether the task measures the intended capability. Reliability asks whether judgements are consistent. Fairness asks whether irrelevant factors—handwriting, accent, confidence, cultural style, disability, technology access—distort performance. Feasibility asks whether students and markers can complete the work to the stated standard.

No single task can assess everything well. The course therefore combines written analysis, case formulation, evidence appraisal, observed practice, reflective commentary, and invigilated or time-bound synthesis where required by institutional rules.

## 2. Proposed Assessment Pattern

Subject to institutional approval, a balanced pattern is:

| Assessment | Weight | Primary capability |
|---|---:|---|
| Theory comparison essay | 20% | Conceptual understanding and critical comparison |
| Evidence appraisal brief | 15% | Research literacy and application to youth |
| Case conceptualisation and treatment plan | 30% | Integrative formulation and planning |
| Observed skills practice with rationale | 20% | Theory-informed intervention and reflection |
| Professional learning portfolio | 15% | Development, ethics, culture, and self-assessment |

A final examination may replace or supplement one component where regulations require. If used, examination questions should require application to unseen cases, not only recall.

## 3. Assignment One: Theory Comparison Essay

**Task.** Compare two theories in relation to adolescent anxiety, depression, school refusal, family conflict, trauma, or another approved presentation. Address view of the person, causation, change, counsellor role, techniques, development, culture, evidence, limitations, and suitability.

**What excellence looks like.** The essay goes beyond parallel summaries. It identifies genuine points of commensurability and conflict, uses evidence accurately, considers a concrete adolescent example, and reaches a justified conclusion.

**Common weaknesses.** Biography replacing analysis; listing techniques without mechanisms; treating evidence as vote counting; assuming adult findings transfer unchanged to adolescents; ignoring family, school, and culture.

## 4. Assignment Two: Evidence Appraisal Brief

**Task.** Select a research article, systematic review, or clinical guideline relevant to one theory or intervention. Appraise question, design, sample, measures, analysis, effect size, limitations, applicability, and implications for youth practice in no more than the assigned length.

Students should distinguish statistical significance from clinical importance and efficacy under trial conditions from effectiveness in ordinary services. They should identify who was excluded from the study and what the intervention required in staffing, training, and time.

A short recommendation section must state: use, adapt, use with caution, or do not use—plus what outcome would be monitored locally.

## 5. Assignment Three: Case Conceptualisation and Treatment Plan

**Task.** Using a provided complex case, produce a developmental, cultural, relational, and evidence-informed formulation and a sequenced treatment plan.

The submission includes:

1. presenting summary and risk;
2. five-part formulation;
3. theory-informed explanation using at least three lenses without incoherent mixing;
4. goals negotiated from client, family, and service perspectives;
5. sequenced interventions with rationale and responsibilities;
6. school, family, health, and safeguarding coordination;
7. outcome measures and review criteria;
8. ethical and cultural analysis;
9. criteria for referral, intensification, or ending.

Word limits reward prioritisation. Appendices may include diagrams and measures but cannot be used to evade core analysis.
