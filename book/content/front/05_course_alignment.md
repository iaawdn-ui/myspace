# Course Alignment: CAC. 701 in the Master of Arts in Children and Adolescent Counseling

## The place of this course in the programme

CAC. 701, *Fundamentals of Counseling Theories*, carries 1.6 units and is taken in the first semester of the Master of Arts in Children and Adolescent Counseling. It is offered alongside CAC. 702 *Child Development Theories*, CAC. 703 *Ethics in Counseling*, CAC. 704 *Counseling Techniques and Skills*, CAC. 705 *Group Counseling for Children and Adolescents*, CAC. 706 *Family Systems Therapy*, CAC. 707 *Play Therapy Techniques*, and CAC. 708 *Fundamentals of Counseling Theories* (a parallel section taught under a second code for the convenience of the Global Extension cohort).

The rationale for placing a theory course in the first semester is straightforward. Every subsequent course in the programme asks students to apply a theory — whether the theory of attachment in CAC. 706, the theory of trauma in CAC. 715, or the theory of behaviour in CAC. 712 — and students who reach those courses without a working command of the major theoretical traditions are reduced to repeating technique lists. CAC. 701 is therefore a foundational course in the intellectual sense as well as the administrative one.

## Course description

This course examines the major theoretical traditions in counseling, with attention to their historical development, their central constructs, the mechanisms of change they propose, the techniques they generate, and their application to the assessment and treatment of children, adolescents, and families. Particular attention is given to the developmental and cultural modifications required when a theory developed with adults is applied to young people, and to the ethical demands that each tradition places on the practitioner. The course addresses both the cognitive and behavioural families, the humanistic and existential traditions, the systemic and family approaches, the emotion-focused and trauma-informed traditions, and the third-wave developments in mindfulness, acceptance, and dialectical practice. The course concludes with integrative formulation and the responsible use of theory in professional practice.

## Course learning outcomes

On successful completion of CAC. 701, students will be able to:

1. **Describe** the major theoretical traditions in counseling, their historical development, and the assumptions about human nature that distinguish them.
2. **Compare** the explanations these traditions give for how difficulties develop and how they are maintained.
3. **Explain** the mechanism of change proposed by each tradition and the techniques that follow from that mechanism.
4. **Apply** one or more theories to a composite case involving a child, adolescent, or family, producing a coherent formulation and treatment plan.
5. **Critically appraise** the evidence base for a selected intervention and identify the limits of that evidence.
6. **Evaluate** the cultural and ethical implications of applying a theory to a young person from a minority or otherwise marginalised background.
7. **Formulate** an integrative stance that is coherent, developmentally appropriate, and defensible to supervision.
8. **Demonstrate** the reflective and relational competencies required to use theory ethically with minors, families, and schools.

## Delivery pattern

CAC. 701 is delivered over a twelve-week semester with three contact hours per week. The standard pattern is a fifty-minute lecture, a fifty-minute reading and discussion seminar, and a sixty-minute skills or application laboratory. Some cohorts deliver the material in two intensive blocks of three days. Appendix F contains session plans and reproducible worksheets for both patterns.
